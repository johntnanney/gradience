# Thesis B — Thesis Memo

*Single-page anchor document preceding §2 revision. April 2026.*

---

*Central claim.* ML diagnostic reporting operates under an implicit view of measurement on which a score is treated as a direct readout of a latent property rather than as a fallible indicator whose relationship to that property must be defended, and its characteristic symptoms — decimal-precision overclaims, absent reliability coefficients, post-hoc confound analysis, undisciplined generalization beyond tested regimes — are not independent methodological failures but coordinated consequences of this implicit view, remediable only by the conceptual reorientation toward inferential measurement that the psychometric tradition has developed since Cronbach & Meehl (1955). The claim is not that ML has the wrong methods and should import correct ones; it is that ML has an implicit philosophy of measurement that generates its methods, and that the philosophy, not the methods in isolation, is what requires revision. The four-component framework this paper proposes is that revision in operational form.

*Four components as jointly constitutive, not independent virtues.* Under the direct-readout view, a score simply reports a quantity and the only measurement question is whether noise has been adequately reduced; reliability is presumed, confounds are second-order worries, and decimal precision follows the computation rather than the data. Under the inferential view the paper defends, a score is an observable whose evidential weight for any substantive claim must be structurally defended, and the defense requires — jointly and inseparably — (i) an account of what the score indicates, i.e., construct validity, operationalized as the distinction between the theoretical object of measurement and the specific computation that instantiates it; (ii) an account of how stable the indication is, i.e., reliability, operationalized as cross-seed ICC or a distribution-based equivalent; (iii) an account of the precision the indication can actually support, i.e., bounded precision, operationalized as an explicit tolerance schedule calibrated to sample size and quantity class; and (iv) an account of what else might explain the signal, i.e., confound decomposition, operationalized as pre-registered residualization with explicit effect-size thresholds. These four are not a toolkit from which an author may pick and choose; they are four aspects of a single commitment to treating scores as indicators, and any defense that omits one tacitly reverts to the direct-readout view at the point of omission. The framework's internal coherence *is* the position; its component methods, considered in isolation, are the ordinary stock of classical test theory and make no contribution to ML at the level of individual techniques.

*Three contributions and their dialectical roles.* The paper's three contributions do distinct argumentative work. The framework (Contribution 1) is the **normative** claim — a proposal for how ML diagnostic reporting should be reorganized, stated at sufficient operational detail that individual authors can adopt it without waiting for field-wide consensus. The worked example (Contribution 2) is the **demonstrative** claim — a pre-registered decoder-scale application showing the framework produces legible scientific content, including a null that a pre-registered decision rule correctly declines to massage into a confirmation; the load-bearing feature is precisely the null, because a framework whose value could only be shown by positive results would be a framework that has not been tested against the outcomes where methodological discipline matters most. The rank-on-residuals observation (Contribution 3) is the **productivity** claim — evidence that measurement discipline is generative rather than merely critical, because the framework's reproducibility-check component surfaced a measurement property of rank-based statistics on small-*N* residuals that unstructured reporting would not have produced and in fact did not. The three contributions together have a specific dialectical shape: normative, demonstrative, productive. A reader who accepts Contribution 1 without Contribution 2 has the framework without evidence that it applies in practice; a reader who accepts Contribution 2 without Contribution 3 has an application of the framework without the epistemic advantage that distinguishes it from alternative methodological proposals; a reader who accepts Contribution 3 without Contribution 1 has an isolated numerical finding without an argument for the general practice. The paper's unity of argument requires all three — not because the three claims are unrelated and mutually supportive in a reviewer-friendly way, but because removing any one collapses the dialectical structure that distinguishes this paper from a findings paper with a methods appendix.
