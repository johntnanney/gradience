## Central question

Given current Route 2 evidence, what should be stated confidently, what should be guarded, and what should remain research-only?

---

## Section 1 -- Stable claims safe for synthesis

Safe to use as core Route 2 synthesis language:

1. Evidence gating and conservative narrowing are cross-artifact workflow invariants in tested scope.
2. Aggregation is a first-class decision seam.
3. Worst-case, distributional, and QA-dominant are genuinely distinct operational families.
4. Workflow-level portability is stronger than metric-level portability.
5. The broadened substrate is real but intentionally narrow.

---

## Section 2 -- Moderately stable claims needing guardrails

Use with explicit caveats:

1. Same-task vs cross-task separation is directionally useful, but class parity is incomplete.
2. Same-family intermediate and same-family optional states are meaningful, but thresholds/orderings remain guarded.
3. Aggregation taxonomy is usable at coarse grain, not as fixed numeric boundaries.
4. Behavioral profile distinctions are useful, but broader artifact-level behavioral replication remains limited.

---

## Section 3 -- Thin or local claims

Keep out of broad external claims:

1. Cross-artifact optional/near-miss portability outside LoRA (thin).
2. Routing-confusability behavioral non-transfer claims (thin).
3. Collapse-vs-contamination as a broad cross-context law (currently local to merge-facing evidence).

---

## Section 4 -- Claims best strengthened next

If additional strengthening work is needed, highest value targets are:

1. Broader non-LoRA optional/near-miss coverage.
2. Scenario-correct behavioral routing evidence beyond merge-setting inference.
3. Additional bounded behavioral replication across artifact classes.

---

## Section 5 -- Communication guidance

### Public language (blog/HF)

Use stable workflow and aggregation seam claims with explicit bounded-scope phrasing.

### Product and alpha workflow language

Use stable claims plus moderate middle-state claims with guardrails; avoid hard thresholds.

### Internal architecture language

Include representation-locality and aggregation-family distinctions explicitly.

### Sidecar-only language

Keep thin/local claims in sidecar context until stronger evidence exists.

---

## Bottom line

The ladder supports stronger Route 2 communication with better calibration: sturdy seam-level and workflow-level claims can be stated plainly, while middle-state and behavioral transfer claims remain useful but bounded.
