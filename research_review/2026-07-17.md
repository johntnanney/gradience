### 1. He et al. (2026), *Compress then Merge: From Multiple LoRAs into One Low-Rank Adapter* — HIGH

**Authors / venue / date:** Zhengbao He, Ruiqi Ding, Zhehao Huang, Ruikai Yang, Tao Li, Xiaolin Huang. **ICML 2026** (published). arXiv preprint submitted June 2, 2026.

**arXiv:** [arXiv:2606.03723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03723)

**One-sentence summary:** Studies the problem of merging T LoRA adapters into a single rank-r LoRA by computing shared r-dimensional subspaces from adapter weights alone, projecting each adapter into these shared bases, applying standard merging rules, and guaranteeing rank-r output by construction — avoiding post-hoc truncation entirely.

**Connection to program:** This is the most directly relevant new work for Gradience's core spectral triage pipeline. The paper formalizes and constructively exploits the exact structural premise that Gradience's `SubspaceMetrics`, principal angle analysis (Bjorck-Golub), and spectral compatibility diagnostics (`vnext/merge/spectral_compat.py`) are designed to measure: that the quality of a LoRA merge depends on the shared subspace structure between adapters. Where Gradience answers "should we merge?" via spectral diagnosis, this paper answers "how to merge given subspace structure" via constructive subspace projection. The shared-subspace computation step is essentially a constructive implementation of what Gradience's overlap metrics (mean/max principal angle cosines, directional agreement, SV-weighted alignment) quantify diagnostically. The "rank-r by construction" guarantee also connects to Gradience's rank suggestion system (`vnext/rank_suggestion.py`) — the merged adapter's rank is known in advance from the subspace computation, not discovered post-hoc.

**Why this criticality tier:** HIGH. Published at ICML 2026 — the field's top venue. Validates the theoretical premise underlying Gradience's entire merge triage system at the highest-credibility level. The convergence is structural: Gradience measures subspace compatibility to decide *whether* to merge; Compress-then-Merge uses the same subspace structure to decide *how* to merge. This positions the two as complementary rather than competing — Gradience's triage decides which pairs are worth merging; Compress-then-Merge provides a merge execution path that respects the subspace structure Gradience diagnoses. A reviewer of Gradience's technical report who is familiar with this ICML paper will expect engagement.

**Relevance to Gradience research lines:**
- **Merge triage pipeline** (primary): validates the spectral compatibility premise
- **Spectral partitioning** (secondary): the shared-subspace computation relates to Gradience's finding that adapter spectra partition into shared vs. task-specific bands
- **Rank policies** (tertiary): rank-r-by-construction connects to rank suggestion framework

**Integration criticality: HIGH — approaching MUST-CITE for the technical report.** The technical report (`docs/technical-report.md`) should engage this paper in its related-work section. The convergence between Gradience's diagnostic approach and this constructive approach strengthens the case that spectral subspace structure is the right signal for merge decisions. The program's "triage, not execution" positioning (THEORY.md §8) is preserved — Compress-then-Merge is execution-side work that validates the measurement substrate.

**Recommended action:** Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` Section 4 at HIGH tier. Stage for technical report related-work engagement. Consider as replication target: what does Gradience's triage system predict about the quality of Compress-then-Merge outputs across pair types (SAFE, REDUNDANT, CONFLICTING, IMBALANCED)?

### 2. Truong Xuan Khanh (2026), *At-Grok Is Not Converged: A Measurement-Validity Audit for Grokking Representation Metrics* — HIGH

**Authors / venue / date:** Truong Xuan Khanh (single author). arXiv preprint submitted July 2026.

**arXiv:** [arXiv:2607.06639](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06639)

**One-sentence summary:** Shows that neural network embeddings continue compressing for tens of thousands of steps after generalization onset, so representation metrics read at the grokking transition overstate converged circuit complexity by 3–5x; LayerNorm shifts the fraction of compression completed by the grok step from 0.87 down to 0.25; provides a measurement-validity audit protocol that separates onset detection from compression convergence.

**Connection to program:** This paper independently applies a measurement-validity audit framework to training-dynamics metrics — the exact epistemological stance that makes N134 distinctive and that Gradience's psychometric approach to spectral diagnostics embodies. The paper's central finding — that reading spectral/representation metrics at a phase transition boundary produces systematically inflated values relative to converged measurements — is precisely the kind of measurement-instrument calibration concern that Gradience's ICC analysis, standard error of measurement, and minimal detectable change infrastructure are designed to address. The LayerNorm sensitivity finding (shifting compression completion from 0.87 to 0.25 at grok-step) is a confound-calibration result: the same metric reads differently depending on architectural context, which is exactly the class of measurement-condition variance that the psychometric stance flags as requiring explicit quantification.

**Why this criticality tier:** HIGH. This is the first external work that explicitly frames training-dynamics metrics as measurement instruments requiring a validity audit — independently converging on Gradience's foundational philosophical commitment. The 3–5x overstatement finding is a concrete, quantified measurement artifact with practical implications for anyone using spectral metrics for training-phase detection (including Gradience's own `research/phase_transitions.py` module and the grokking experiments in `experiments/grokking*/`). The paper's audit protocol (separating onset from convergence) is methodologically adjacent to Gradience's test-retest reliability analysis.

**Relevance to Gradience research lines:**
- **Measurement discipline / N134** (primary): independent convergence on the measurement-validity framework; direct engagement target for future-revision bibliography
- **Phase transition detection** (secondary): calibration concern for Gradience's own grokking findings (FINDING_phase_transition.txt, FINDING_rank_order_parameter.txt)
- **Spectral diagnostics reliability** (tertiary): the 3–5x inflation finding has implications for the program's ICC analysis of spectral metrics across seeds

**Integration criticality: HIGH — should be cited in N134 at first revision.** The convergence validates the program's fundamental epistemological commitment. For N134, the paper serves as an external demonstration of the measurement-validity stance on a different substrate (grokking representation metrics vs. spectral audit diagnostics), strengthening the case that measurement discipline is a general-purpose methodology, not a special-purpose tool. The specific findings about LayerNorm sensitivity and onset-vs-convergence separation should be checked against Gradience's own grokking telemetry in `experiments/grokking_long/` — if the 0.87→0.25 pattern replicates in Gradience's effective-rank trajectories, that would be a convergent-operations result.

**Recommended action:** Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` Section 4 at HIGH tier. Stage for N134 first-revision bibliography as an external convergence citation. Run a focused check: do Gradience's grokking effective-rank trajectories show continued compression past the generalization onset point? If so, quantify the at-onset vs. converged ratio and compare to the 3–5x range reported.

### 3. Feng, Lin, & Kao (2026), *Learning in the Fisher Subspace: A Guided Initialization for LoRA Fine-Tuning* (FILet) — MEDIUM-HIGH

**Authors / venue / date:** Zhi-Quan Feng, Ying-Jia Lin, Hung-Yu Kao. arXiv preprint submitted May 1, 2026.

**arXiv:** [arXiv:2605.01046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01046)

**One-sentence summary:** Formulates LoRA initialization as the problem of identifying high-impact directions in parameter space under the target data distribution, proposing a Fisher-Energy-guided framework that approximates the Hessian via the empirical Fisher information matrix to select LoRA adaptation subspaces aligned with convergence-favorable curvature directions; especially effective for math and code generation tasks.

**Connection to program:** THEORY.md §3 (Information Geometry) explicitly hypothesizes that `erank(ΔW)` probes the effective dimensionality of the task-relevant subspace of the Fisher manifold. Gradience has a full Fisher information research module (`gradience/research/fisher.py`) that computes empirical Fisher diagonal, spectral properties, natural gradient alignment, and effective dimensionality. FILet provides the first direct empirical evidence that Fisher energy — the same curvature signal Gradience's Fisher module tracks — can successfully guide the *selection* of the adaptation subspace, not just characterize it post-hoc. This bridges two of Gradience's own research threads: the spectral audit pipeline (which measures the subspace that training discovers) and the information-geometric perspective (which theorizes about *why* training discovers that particular subspace). FILet's finding that Fisher-guided initialization outperforms random and magnitude-based initialization is empirical support for the hypothesis that the Fisher manifold's geometry causally determines which spectral directions are task-relevant — moving Gradience's open question from "hypothesized" toward "empirically supported by independent work."

**Why this criticality tier:** MEDIUM-HIGH, not HIGH. The connection to Gradience is genuine but indirect: FILet operates on initialization (before training), while Gradience's Fisher module operates on post-training analysis (after training). The finding validates the information-geometric hypothesis but does not directly affect Gradience's current product or manuscripts. The paper's empirical scope (math and code tasks on a few architectures) is narrower than Gradience's ecosystem-scale validation (86 adapters, 22 architectures). Promoted above MEDIUM because: (a) the Fisher-spectral connection is one of Gradience's explicitly stated open theoretical questions; (b) two companion papers in the same period — FoRA (arXiv:2605.29317, Fisher-orthogonal rank adaptation, May 2026) and FIM-LoRA (arXiv:2605.16800, Fisher-based rank allocation, May 2026) — form a cluster of three independent Fisher-guided-LoRA papers in May 2026, suggesting the field is converging on Fisher information as the right lens for rank/subspace selection, which validates Gradience's research thread.

**Relevance to Gradience research lines:**
- **Information geometry / Fisher connection** (primary): direct empirical support for THEORY.md §3 hypothesis
- **Rank policies** (secondary): Fisher-guided subspace selection is a new candidate rank policy family
- **Training dynamics** (tertiary): curvature-guided initialization connects to Gradience's curvature telemetry findings about Hessian energy leading validation accuracy

**Integration criticality: MEDIUM-HIGH — cite in THEORY.md §3 update and FINDINGS.md as convergent evidence.** Not action-forcing for current manuscripts (N134 and N135 do not engage the Fisher thread), but strengthens the theoretical framework's open-question resolution trajectory. The cluster of three Fisher-LoRA papers (FILet + FoRA + FIM-LoRA) in May 2026 suggests a field-level convergence that the program should acknowledge in its theory documentation.

**Recommended action:** Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` Section 4 at MEDIUM-HIGH tier. Stage for THEORY.md §3 update noting the Fisher-LoRA convergence cluster. Consider whether Gradience's Fisher module could be extended to produce initialization recommendations (moving from post-hoc diagnosis to pre-training prescription), which would align the program with this emerging direction.
