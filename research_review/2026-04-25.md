**arXiv / DOI:** [arXiv:2604.11581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11581) · [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2604.11581](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2604.11581)

**One-sentence summary:** Decomposes LLM evaluation-pipeline uncertainty into design-choice variance vs. shrinking-with-N variance, derives confidence intervals that approach nominal coverage from a small-sample pilot, and demonstrates on MMLU benchmarking that optimized budget allocation halves estimation error at equivalent cost.

**Connection to program:** Directly parallel to the benchmark reliability study (locked today). **Why this criticality tier:** HIGH not CRITICAL. However, the papers occupy adjacent intellectual territory and were developed in parallel during the same six-week window. The benchmark reliability study's manuscript will need to engage Messing in related work and Discussion, and the N134 paper's claim that measurement-discipline-for-ML-diagnostics is novel deserves a recalibration: a parallel reformulation has been published as a preprint, framed in different vocabulary but reaching adjacent conclusions. This recalibration is editorial, not invalidating.

**Recommended action:** Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` as HIGH (parallel-development, must-cite). Add to manuscript citation list for both papers (N134 introduction; benchmark reliability study related-work, §3.1, and Discussion). Consider explicit positioning paragraph distinguishing G-theory + tolerance schedule (Gradience) from TEE + design-study projection (Messing) — the two are complementary framings of the same diagnosis, and naming this complementarity is stronger than ignoring it. **Do not modify** the v1.1.1 pre-registration; this is paper-positioning only, not protocol-revising.

**Framework-applied note:** Messing's paper does engage measurement-discipline norms — it reports confidence-interval coverage explicitly, decomposes variance components, and validates across multiple substrates. The framework's recursive scrutiny does not flag obvious violations from the abstract alone; full-text review would be needed to assess whether reliability of the small-sample pilot itself is reported with the same rigor. ### NIST AI 800-3 — *Expanding the AI Evaluation Toolbox with Statistical Models* — HIGH

**Authors / venue / date:** NIST Information Technology Laboratory + CAISI. Published February 2026.

**arXiv / DOI:** [NIST news release](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/02/new-report-expanding-ai-evaluation-toolbox-statistical-models) · [PDF](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.800-3.pdf)

**One-sentence summary:** Demonstrates a generalized linear mixed model (GLMM) approach to AI benchmark evaluation, decomposing variance into between-question and within-question components on 22 frontier LLMs across GPQA-Diamond, BIG-Bench Hard, and Global-MMLU Lite.

**Connection to program:** Direct methodological overlap with Analysis 1 (variance components decomposition via mixed-effects logistic regression at item level) of the benchmark reliability study. NIST's GLMM-based approach is methodologically sympathetic with the spec's §10.1 specification (which calls for binomial-family logistic GLMMs). However, the benchmark study's *implementation* uses a Linear Probability Model (LPM) per implementation deviation D-09 — `statsmodels.MixedLM` is Gaussian-only, and the spec's cited library does not implement the spec's cited model. NIST's formal endorsement of GLMMs raises the bar for what a methodologically-disciplined manuscript should report. The MMLU overlap is also direct: the benchmark study's primary panel includes MMLU subjects; NIST evaluated Global-MMLU Lite. The vocabularies are different — NIST uses GLMMs and IRT-style ability parameters; the benchmark study uses G-theory variance components and generalizability coefficients — but both are valid psychometric frameworks for the same underlying decomposition.

**Why this criticality tier:** HIGH, not CRITICAL. The benchmark study's contribution is not subsumed: (1) NIST does not formulate the decimal-place precision tolerance schedule; (2) NIST's empirical scope is frontier closed models, while the benchmark study scopes to small-to-mid-scale open models — explicit non-overlap (§9.1 of prereg); (3) the methodological vocabularies (GLMM-IRT vs G-theory) differ. However, the implementation deviation D-09 (LPM substituted for GLMM) is now in tension with NIST's formal endorsement of the GLMM approach. The manuscript's Analysis 1 discussion needs to either (a) defend LPM as an adequate proxy under benchmark accuracy ranges (current D-09 justification: variance proportions match within a few percent for accuracies in 0.15–0.85), or (b) commit to the rpy2 + lme4 path. The current D-09 justification is defensible but should be more sharply argued in light of NIST 800-3.

**Recommended action:** Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` as **HIGH** (parallel-development, must-cite for benchmark reliability study). Add to manuscript citation list (introduction, related work, Analysis 1 discussion). Strengthen the D-09 deviation's manuscript-facing justification (`IMPLEMENTATION_DEVIATIONS.md` already names this for v1.1 update; NIST 800-3 increases the urgency). Possible follow-up methodological appendix: a side-by-side comparison of LPM and logistic GLMM variance proportions on the actual benchmark study data, demonstrating that the methodological substitution is innocuous for the tolerance-schedule output. **Do not modify** the v1.1.1 pre-registration; the LPM choice is locked.

**Framework-applied note:** NIST 800-3 is itself a measurement-discipline-flavored document — variance decomposition, IRT-style ability estimation, latent-capability framing all align with the framework's register.
