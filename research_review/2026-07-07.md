### 1. Park, Bocchi, D'Amico, Lucini, Aarts (June 2026). *Spectral phase transitions and trainability in neural network learning dynamics.* arXiv:2606.28486

**Tier recommendation:** HIGH (approaching MUST-CITE for Thesis A if phase-transition thread is revived)

**Summary:** Formulates neural network training as the stochastic evolution of an initially random matrix ensemble driven by SGD updates that reshape the spectral bulk while amplifying signal strength. Demonstrates that training induces a Baik-Ben Arous-Péché (BBP) transition where isolated eigenvalues detach from the random bulk distribution. Derives an analytically tractable phase diagram of trainability governed by learning rate and initial weight variance in a linear teacher-student model. The BBP transition provides a dynamical framework for representation formation in high-dimensional learning dynamics.

**Relevance to Gradience:**

- **Direct connection to open question #6** (THEORY.md): "Does Hessian curvature collapse coincide with Marchenko-Pastur spectral partition sharpening?" This paper provides the mathematical machinery (BBP transition as the mechanism by which signal eigenvalues detach from the MP bulk) that could formalize this conjecture. Gradience already uses the Marchenko-Pastur distribution (via Gavish-Donoho optimal hard threshold) in its rank policies — this paper extends the same random-matrix-theory substrate to *training dynamics*, where Gradience has empirical findings (Hessian energy leads validation by 3-6 updates, three-act gradient alignment structure) but lacks formal theory.
- **Phase transition detection:** Gradience's `research/phase_transitions.py` implements critical-slowing-down and grokking detectors. The BBP framework provides a spectral criterion for when training "works" — when the signal eigenvalue exits the bulk. This is a more fundamental phase transition than the behavioral ones Gradience currently detects (accuracy jumps, loss plateaus), and could serve as a leading indicator.
- **Spectral partitioning connection:** Gradience's empirical finding that high-SV bands concentrate inter-task alignment (89% in top 20% by magnitude) while low-SV bands encode task-specific features (only 3% alignment) maps naturally onto the BBP framework's distinction between "detached outlier eigenvalues" (signal) and "bulk spectrum" (noise/task-specific structure).

**Criticality assessment:** HIGH. This paper provides the theoretical backbone that would connect Gradience's two currently-separate research threads (spectral analysis + phase transition detection) into a unified framework. The BBP transition is the mathematical object that describes *when* spectral structure becomes detectable — exactly the question Gradience's monitoring pipeline needs answered to move from post-hoc analysis to predictive triage. If the program revives the training-dynamics thread (deferred per options memo), this becomes MUST-CITE. Even without revival, it belongs in N134's related-work as the formal theory underlying the spectral phenomena the framework measures.

### 2. Tian, Chen, Han, Liao (March 2026). *Spectral Surgery: Training-Free Refinement of LoRA via Gradient-Guided Singular Value Reweighting.* arXiv:2603.03995

**Tier recommendation:** HIGH

**Summary:** Proposes a training-free post-hoc refinement that decomposes a trained LoRA update via SVD, estimates per-singular-component sensitivity using gradients on a small calibration set, and reweights singular values under a magnitude constraint while keeping the learned singular directions fixed. Key empirical finding: trained LoRA updates exhibit a consistent *subspace-spectrum dichotomy* — the learned singular subspaces are comparatively stable and task-aligned, but the learned spectrum (singular value magnitudes) can be inefficient or even detrimental, with task effects concentrating in a small subset of singular directions while remaining components are neutral or harmful. Yields up to +4.4 points on CommonsenseQA and +2.4 pass@1 on HumanEval across Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B.

**Relevance to Gradience:**

- **Direct empirical validation of Gradience's core thesis.** Gradience's 86-adapter survey found mean rank utilization of 0.172 (adapters use ~1/6 of allocated rank). Spectral Surgery confirms this from the opposite direction — they find the spectrum is "inefficient" and demonstrate that fixing it (without retraining) yields measurable gains. The 82% "dead weight" figure in Gradience's analysis corresponds to Spectral Surgery's "neutral or detrimental" singular components.
- **Subspace-spectrum dichotomy validates spectral partitioning.** Gradience's finding that high-SV bands concentrate shared structure while low-SV bands encode task-specifics is the *same structural observation* reframed as "subspaces are stable, spectrum is the bottleneck." This convergence from independent research strengthens the evidentiary basis for Gradience's spectral audit approach.
- **Actionable downstream application of spectral audit.** Gradience's `gradience audit` measures spectral properties; Spectral Surgery demonstrates that acting on those measurements (reweighting the spectrum) produces concrete improvements. This creates a natural pipeline: Gradience diagnoses → Spectral Surgery (or similar) treats. If Gradience ever adds a "fix" mode alongside its "audit" mode, this paper's method is the template.
- **Calibration-set sensitivity estimation** is methodologically relevant to Gradience's reliability framework — the gradient-based importance score is itself a measurement instrument with psychometric properties (stability across calibration sets, sensitivity to calibration-set composition).

**Criticality assessment:** HIGH. This is the strongest external empirical validation of Gradience's rank-underutilization thesis published since the program's inception. The convergence on "subspaces stable, spectrum inefficient" from independent research is exactly the kind of replication evidence that strengthens N134's empirical claims. Should be cited in N134 as convergent evidence in §3 or §6, and tracked as a potential downstream-application partner. The gradient-sensitivity method also raises a framework-applied question: what is the test-retest reliability of the gradient-based importance scores across different calibration sets? This is the kind of recursive scrutiny the program's measurement-discipline framework was designed to perform.

### 3. Coeurdoux, Ferré, Bouchaud (April 2026). *Random Matrix Theory of Early-Stopped Gradient Flow: A Transient BBP Scenario.* arXiv:2604.18450

**Tier recommendation:** MEDIUM-HIGH

**Summary:** Provides an analytically tractable random-matrix model for a transient regime in which signal is detectable in a finite gradient descent time window before overfitting dominates. In a linear teacher-student setting with anisotropic input covariance, learning occurs when an isolated eigenvalue separates from a noisy bulk (a BBP transition), before eventually disappearing in the overfitting regime. The key ingredient is anisotropy in the input covariance, which induces fast and slow directions in the learning dynamics. Derives the full time-dependent bulk spectrum via a 2x2 Dyson equation and obtains an explicit outlier condition for a rank-one teacher via a rank-two determinant formula.

**Relevance to Gradience:**

- **Transient signal windows map onto Gradience's Hessian lead finding.** Gradience discovered that Hessian energy leads validation accuracy by 3-6 updates during LoRA fine-tuning. This paper provides the formal mechanism: the signal eigenvalue enters and exits detectability in a finite window, and the window's duration is governed by the covariance anisotropy. The "3-6 update lead" could be the gap between when the BBP transition occurs in curvature space vs. when it manifests in accuracy.
- **Fast/slow learning directions connect to spectral band structure.** The paper's anisotropic covariance → fast/slow decomposition parallels Gradience's high-SV/low-SV band partitioning. The fast directions (corresponding to high-eigenvalue covariance blocks) learn first and overfit first; the slow directions (low-eigenvalue blocks) carry the durable task-specific signal. This provides a theoretical explanation for why Gradience observes that low-SV bands are more task-specific.
- **Early-stopping criterion from spectral dynamics.** The paper's finding that signal is only detectable in a finite window has implications for Gradience's monitoring pipeline: the optimal monitoring window for spectral diagnostics may be bounded, and Gradience's telemetry should track when the "outlier eigenvalue" appears and disappears.
- **Complementary to arXiv:2606.28486:** Together, these two papers (Park et al. on persistent BBP transitions, Coeurdoux et al. on transient BBP scenarios) bracket the theoretical space Gradience's empirical observations occupy — some spectral structure persists (shared high-SV band) while other structure is transient (task-specific learning windows).

**Criticality assessment:** MEDIUM-HIGH. More theoretical and model-specific (linear teacher-student) than the other two recommendations, but provides the formal mechanism behind Gradience's most striking empirical finding (Hessian-curvature lead). The Bouchaud authorship (Jean-Philippe Bouchaud, Capital Fund Management / École Polytechnique) carries significant weight in the random-matrix-theory community. If the program revives training-dynamics work, this paper and arXiv:2606.28486 together provide the theoretical substrate. For current execution, cite-if-natural in FINDINGS.md §16a (Hessian-weight co-evolution) as the formal mechanism for the transient-signal phenomenon.
