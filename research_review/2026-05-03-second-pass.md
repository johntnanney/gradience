### Hochlehnert, Bhatnagar, Udandarao, Albanie, Prabhu, Bethge (2025), *A Sober Look at Progress in Language Model Reasoning: Pitfalls and Paths to Reproducibility* — MEDIUM

### Yuan et al. (2025), *Give Me FP32 or Give Me Death? / Understanding and Mitigating Numerical Sources of Nondeterminism in LLM Inference* — MEDIUM

**Authors / venue / date:** Yuan et al. (Author cluster from arXiv ADS abstract — full author list to verify at citation-staging time). arXiv preprint **submitted June 2025**. **Selected for oral presentation at NeurIPS 2025**. Companion repo: [nanomaoli/llm_reproducibility](https://github.com/nanomaoli/llm_reproducibility).

**arXiv / DOI:** [arXiv:2506.09501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09501) · [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09501v2) · [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Q3qAsZAEZw) · [NeurIPS 2025 poster page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/118169) · [v1 title: "Give Me FP32 or Give Me Death? Challenges and Solutions for Reproducible Reasoning"](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09501v1)

**One-sentence summary:** Systematic investigation of how numerical precision (FP32 / FP16 / BF16), evaluation batch size, GPU count, and GPU architecture affect LLM-output reproducibility, finding that the small gap between competing logits makes token selection vulnerable to minute numerical fluctuations and proposing LayerCast — a lightweight inference pipeline that stores weights in 16-bit but performs all computations in FP32.

**Connection to program:** Direct register engagement with a variance source the benchmark reliability study explicitly does **not** decompose. The benchmark study's measurement universe (§4.2) covers prompt × few-shot seed × scoring rule; hardware, GPU configuration, and inference precision are pre-conditions of the locked spec (one transformers version, full-precision/bfloat16 inference per §5.1) rather than measurement facets. This paper demonstrates that those pre-conditions *also* contribute non-trivial variance to benchmark accuracy — particularly for reasoning-style tasks where minor rounding cascades into divergent chains-of-thought. The N134 paper's "rank-on-residuals precision observation" engaged float32 precision in *correlation computations on small-N residuals*, a different precision domain than this paper's *inference-time floating-point precision*; the two findings share a register (floating-point precision is non-trivial for ML measurement) but not a substrate.

**Why this criticality tier:** MEDIUM. NeurIPS 2025 Oral acceptance is strong evidential weight, and the variance source is genuinely orthogonal to the benchmark study's measurement universe. **Not HIGH** because: (a) the variance source is *outside* the benchmark study's deliberately-bounded measurement universe — the §4.1 admissibility rule treats hardware/precision-format as pre-conditions, and this is a defensible scope decision rather than a coverage gap requiring methodological revision; (b) the paper's prescriptive output (LayerCast inference pipeline) is an inference-engineering contribution, not a measurement-instrument contribution the program would adopt; (c) protocol is locked at v1.1.2 — adopting an additional variance facet would require re-running Phase 4 inference. **Not LOW** because: (a) NeurIPS 2025 Oral venue acceptance is the strongest tier; (b) the paper provides the canonical reference for a §13 (Limitations) acknowledgement that the benchmark study's measurement universe deliberately excludes hardware/precision-format variance; (c) the BF16 finding is operationally relevant to the benchmark study's GPU runs (which used bfloat16 where standard, per §5.1) and would be relevant to a future replication considering FP32 controls.

**Recommended action:** Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` Section 4 — Tracked parallel work — at **MEDIUM** tier (or potentially as a new sub-register entry on "variance sources outside the program's measurement universe"). Cite-if-natural in benchmark reliability study **§13 (Limitations)** as the canonical reference for the hardware/precision-format scope decision — name explicitly that the locked measurement universe excludes hardware/precision-format variance and that Yuan et al. NeurIPS 2025 demonstrates this is a non-trivial source. Cite-if-natural in N134 §1.2 footnote alongside the rank-on-residuals precision observation, as evidence that floating-point precision matters for ML measurement at multiple registers. **No protocol implications at v1.1.2** — the variance source is deliberately outside the locked measurement universe.

**Framework-applied note:** The paper provides quantitative claims (FP32 near-perfect determinism; FP16 moderate stability; BF16 significant variance) with empirical scope bounds (hardware/software/precision settings systematically tested). The methodology survives a measurement-discipline scrutiny check at the abstract level — claim scope is appropriately named and the LayerCast prescription is empirically motivated. Worth flagging at citation-staging time that the paper's variance-source identification is methodologically aligned with the benchmark study's variance-decomposition register (different facet, same underlying argument that benchmark accuracy is a sample from a measurement design). Recursive framework-applied opportunity is light; the paper engages reproducibility evidence directly.

### Authors-not-yet-confirmed (2025), *Flaw or Artifact? Rethinking Prompt Sensitivity in Evaluating LLMs* — HIGH

**Authors / venue / date:** Author cluster to verify at citation-staging time (search did not return full authorship). **Accepted to EMNLP 2025 Main Conference** (per [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1006/) and [OpenReview venue ID frH3KtOZPC](https://openreview.net/forum?id=frH3KtOZPC)). arXiv preprint **submitted September 2025**.

**arXiv / DOI:** [arXiv:2509.01790](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01790) · [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.01790v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.01790) · [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1006/) · [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=frH3KtOZPC) · [HuggingFace paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.01790)

**One-sentence summary:** Empirical study across 7 LLMs (GPT and Gemini families) × 6 benchmarks × 12 prompt templates arguing that **much of the widely-reported prompt sensitivity in LLM evaluation is an artifact of heuristic evaluation methods (log-likelihood scoring and rigid answer matching) rather than an inherent model weakness**, and demonstrating that LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation substantially reduces performance variance and produces consistently higher correlation in model rankings across prompts.

**Connection to program:** **Direct counter-position to the benchmark reliability study's H1/H3 framing.** The benchmark study's measurement universe (§4.2) deliberately includes both LL and G&P scoring rules and treats their variance contribution as a measurement-condition facet to decompose. This paper argues (a) that LL and rigid-matching scoring are the *source* of much observed prompt sensitivity, and (b) that LLM-as-a-Judge — explicitly excluded from the benchmark study's admissibility per §4.1 ("an LLM-as-judge that does not follow an explicit community protocol is not admissible in the primary study") — substantially reduces both variance and ranking instability. The H3 finding (5/5 primary benchmarks show ≥20% pairwise reversal under condition resampling) is in tension with this paper's claim that ranking correlation across prompts is much higher under LLM-as-Judge evaluation. The benchmark study's response is implicit but defensible: the §4.1 admissibility rule treats LLM-as-Judge as a different operationalization (different measurement universe) rather than an alternative scoring rule within the same universe. But the response needs to be **explicit** in §2.1 (related work) and §7.4 (ranking-stability discussion) given that this is now a published EMNLP 2025 Main Conference paper directly on the same question. The benchmark study's distinctive prescriptive contribution — the decimal-place precision tolerance schedule — survives this paper unchanged: even granting that LL-vs-G&P scoring variance can be reduced by switching to LLM-as-Judge, the resulting LLM-as-Judge measurements still need a tolerance schedule, and the program's variance-decomposition methodology applies in either measurement universe.

**Why this criticality tier:** HIGH. **EMNLP 2025 Main Conference acceptance** (top-tier venue) and **direct counter-positioning on the benchmark study's central arguments** (H1, H3, scoring-rule facet) make engagement non-optional for the benchmark study's first-revision pass. The 8-voice parallel-development register the program tracks is dominantly *convergent* with the benchmark study's register; this paper is one of the **first explicitly contrarian voices** to surface — arguing that prompt sensitivity is artifact-of-evaluation not a real measurement issue, and that switching to LLM-as-Judge resolves much of it. The benchmark study's framing must engage this counter-position to maintain field-facing defensibility. **Not CRITICAL** because: (a) the protocol is locked at v1.1.2 and the paper does not invalidate any pre-registered hypothesis test (the H3 variance under the benchmark study's measurement universe remains a real finding even if a different measurement universe — LLM-as-Judge — produces less variance); (b) the paper's argument that LL-and-rigid-matching scoring explain much variance is structurally compatible with the benchmark study's framing (the scoring-rule facet is one of three in the measurement universe; the paper just attributes more weight to it); (c) the paper does not affect any execution path; only manuscript revision. **Definitely HIGH** because: the benchmark study's §2.1 related work paragraph and §7.4 discussion both currently lack engagement with this counter-position, and EMNLP 2025 Main Conference acceptance is the strongest evidential weight tier short of a Nature / Science / NeurIPS Oral.

**Recommended action:** Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` Section 4 — Tracked parallel work — at **HIGH** tier, with a note that this is the **first contrarian voice** in the parallel-development register (the eight existing voices are convergent; this is the ninth and a different category). **Action-forcing for the benchmark reliability study's first-revision pass**: §2.1 related work needs a paragraph engaging the counter-position; §4.1 admissibility-rule discussion may need to defend the LLM-as-Judge exclusion explicitly against this paper; §7.4 ranking-stability discussion should acknowledge the contrast (their finding: ranking correlation high under LLM-as-Judge; the benchmark study's finding: ranking reversals high under LL/G&P measurement universe). For N134, low-priority — this is benchmark-study-substrate-specific and not directly relevant to the LoRA spectral-triage worked example. **Closes a 2025-Q3 backlog window catch** that the inaugural through 2026-05-02 daily reviews missed despite being in the canonical EMNLP submission window. **Do not regard as a methodological threat** — the benchmark study's findings under its declared measurement universe remain valid; this paper simply argues for a different measurement universe (LLM-as-Judge). The framing response is to name LLM-as-Judge as an alternative measurement universe whose variance properties this paper characterizes, while preserving the benchmark study's contribution as the tolerance-schedule prescription for LL/G&P universes.

**Framework-applied note:** The paper makes quantitative claims about variance reduction and ranking correlation under LLM-as-Judge vs LL/G&P scoring across 7 LLMs × 6 benchmarks × 12 prompts. **Critical gap:** the abstract framing does not specify whether the LLM-as-Judge variance reduction itself comes with reliability bounds (which judge model? what is the inter-judge reliability? does the variance reduction generalize across judge architectures?). Under measurement-discipline scrutiny, the paper's claim that "LLM-as-a-Judge resolves prompt sensitivity" requires evidence that the LLM-as-Judge measurement universe is *itself* reliable across judge variation — which is the same kind of recursive scrutiny BenchRisk and JudgeSense engage. Full-text review at citation-staging time would clarify whether the paper measures this. This is a **direct candidate for the framework-as-meta-tool register** the inventory's Section 8 note 2 advocates: a paper that argues for switching measurement universes should itself report the new measurement universe's reliability properties before the field adopts the prescription.
