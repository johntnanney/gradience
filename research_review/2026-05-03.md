**Authors / venue / date:** Olawale Salaudeen (MIT), Anka Reuel, Ahmed Ahmed, Suhana Bedi, Zachary Robertson, Sudharsan Sundar, Ben Domingue, Angelina Wang, Sanmi Koyejo (MIT / Stanford). arXiv preprint, **v1 submitted 2025-05-13, v4 last revised 2025-06-26**, CC-BY-NC-ND 4.0. **One-sentence summary:** Provides a structured psychometric-validity-decomposition framework for AI evaluation that maps available evidence to the kinds of evaluative claims it can support, illustrated with case studies in vision and language model evaluations and grounded in psychometrics' breakdown of validity facets.

**Connection to program:** Direct engagement with the same load-bearing register N134 occupies. The paper "leverages psychometrics' breakdown of validity" to inform which evaluative claims a given evaluation supports — structurally identical to N134's §1.2 / §2 argument that ML diagnostics should adopt the construct-validity register Messick formulates and Cronbach-Meehl ground. Two specific connections beyond topical overlap. (1) **Author overlap with the program's existing register:** Anka Reuel is the second author here and the program already engages Reuel through BenchRisk (McGregor et al. NeurIPS 2025, Section 4 HIGH); this paper is the additional Reuel-coauthored title in the AI-evaluation-validity space alongside BenchRisk. Ben Domingue is a Stanford psychometrician — the kind of psychometric-discipline-internal author whose presence in an ML paper is itself a parallel-development-register signal. (2) **Methodological positioning:** the paper's argument that evaluations should be designed to speak to validity facets (rather than reporting accuracy as a generic capability claim) is the same critique N134 makes specifically against three- and four-decimal-precision reporting without reliability or precision infrastructure. The papers are in the same argumentative tradition; the present program's distinctive contribution (decimal-place precision tolerance schedule) is preserved, but the framework-as-stance is no longer a program-singular intervention.

**Why this criticality tier:** HIGH, not CRITICAL. The program's distinctive prescriptive contributions remain unsupplanted: Salaudeen et al. produce a validity-centered framework for connecting evidence to claims, but do not formulate a decimal-place precision tolerance schedule (N135's contribution) and do not work an ML diagnostic case under preregistration with confound-defeat design (N134's contribution). NeurIPS 2025, Freiesleben 2026) now make adjacent claims, and N134's §1.2 framing as "co-developed register, distinctive prescriptive contribution" is the right register to hold. **HIGH because** (a) it is a 2025-Q2 paper that should have surfaced earlier in the program's coverage and didn't, and the calibration note from yesterday explicitly named 2025-Q2/Q3 backlog-clearing as the targeted-retrospective-query objective; (b) the Reuel author-thread overlap with BenchRisk makes engagement non-optional for both manuscripts at first-revision stage; (c) Ben Domingue's psychometrician pedigree is directly relevant to the framework's psychometrics-port argument and the kind of co-author signal a reviewer will expect engagement with. ### Greenberg, Hall, Jensen, Greene, Amironesei (2025), *Branching Out: Broadening AI Measurement and Evaluation with Measurement Trees* — MEDIUM

**Authors / venue / date:** Craig Greenberg, Patrick Hall, Theodore Jensen, Kristen Greene, Razvan Amironesei. NIST-affiliated authors (Greenberg, Greene at NIST per pedigree consistency with NIST AI 800-2/800-3 already in the inventory). arXiv preprint, submitted **2025-09-30** (cs.LG / stat.ML).

**arXiv / DOI:** [arXiv:2509.26632](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26632) · [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.26632)

**One-sentence summary:** Introduces measurement trees, a hierarchical-aggregation metric class that combines heterogeneous AI-system evaluation evidence (agentic, business, energy-efficiency, sociotechnical, security signals) into an interpretable directed-graph representation, with each node summarizing children via user-defined aggregation methods.

**Connection to program:** Adjacency to the program's existing NIST 800-2/800-3 MUST-CITE alignment. The benchmark-reliability study's regime-split resolution (D-09 → v1.1.2) and the program's NIST-alignment register that Section 8 Note 1 of `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` calls "increasingly load-bearing" make NIST-team output adjacent to the program's manuscript-positioning concerns. Methodologically, measurement trees are a hierarchical-aggregation construction over heterogeneous evidence — a different inferential target from the benchmark study's variance-components decomposition over a homogeneous accuracy outcome under prompt × seed × scoring-rule resampling. The papers are in the same family (NIST-aligned AI evaluation methodology) but address different questions: measurement trees aggregate heterogeneous evidence streams; the benchmark study decomposes within-cell variance components.

**Why this criticality tier:** MEDIUM, not HIGH. Methodologically distinct from G-theory variance decomposition; the paper's primary contribution (hierarchical-aggregation metric class with open-source Python code) addresses a different decision-context (multi-stream evidence aggregation for sociotechnical evaluation) than the benchmark study's substrate (single-stream accuracy decomposition). Not action-forcing for either manuscript: N134 and N135 do not aggregate heterogeneous evidence streams; they decompose single-stream scoring objects. **Not LOW because** the NIST authorship continues the program's NIST-alignment register and BenchRisk's NIST risk-management process is methodologically adjacent (both use hierarchical-process structures over heterogeneous evidence). **Not HIGH because** the inferential target divergence is structural, not stylistic — measurement trees and tolerance schedules answer different questions. Cite-if-natural in the benchmark study's §9 (prescriptive reporting standard) as the NIST-aligned methodology track that aggregates heterogeneous signals while the present paper's track licenses precision in single-signal reports.

**Recommended action:** Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` Section 4 — Tracked parallel work — at **MEDIUM** tier with note that it is the second NIST-team output the program is tracking (alongside NIST 800-2/800-3). Cite-if-natural in the benchmark-reliability study at §2.1 or §9 as the NIST-aligned heterogeneous-evidence-aggregation companion methodology to the within-cell variance-decomposition track the present study takes; the citation supports the manuscript's positioning as one branch of the broader NIST-aligned AI evaluation methodology family. **No protocol implications** for either manuscript.

**Framework-applied note:** The paper presents definitions, examples, and a large-scale measurement exercise demonstration; per the abstract, it does not report reliability bounds on the measurement-tree aggregation itself. The recursive measurement-discipline question — what reliability does a measurement-tree aggregation have under varying user-defined aggregation methods, what tolerance does it license on the aggregated claim — is the framework-applied scrutiny opportunity. For the program's manuscripts, this is reading-into-record citation rather than methodological engagement; the framework-applied scrutiny would belong to a future-paper that audits NIST-aligned methodology under measurement-discipline standards.
