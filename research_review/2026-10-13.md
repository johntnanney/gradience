## 1. The Intruder Threshold: A Spectral Law for LoRA Fine-Tuning

**Paper:** Peng Xie, arXiv:2607.23711 (July 2026)
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23711

### What it does

Derives a **per-layer critical update strength** from the pretrained weight spectrum alone, with zero fitted parameters. Uses the rectangular spiked-deformation transform from random matrix theory (BBP-type framework) to characterize when a LoRA update's singular values "intrude" into the pretrained spectrum — the point at which the update begins to reshape the layer's representation rather than merely perturbing it.

Validated across **18 adapters and 9,840 layer scans** spanning dense Transformers, a state-space model, a mixture-of-experts model, and an encoder-decoder. The law localizes the empirical threshold within a factor of two on 82% of layers and predicts where WikiText-2 perplexity begins to degrade. A spike-budget rule derived from the thresholds reduces forgetting by 62% on the most fragile model at no task cost.

### Why it matters for Gradience

This paper directly formalizes what Gradience has been empirically observing and addresses multiple open theoretical questions:

- **Concentration-weighted convergence bound (THEORY.md §7.2):** The intruder threshold is computed from W₀'s full spectrum, not adjacent spectral gaps. This is exactly the kind of spectral-mass-derived quantity Gradience needs — recall that N127/N130 showed the naive Davis-Kahan gap (σ₁−σ₂) fails empirically (r = 0.038, p = 0.86), while energy concentration predicts alignment (r = 0.53–0.58). The intruder threshold is a candidate formal object for the bound.

- **Compression safety (THEORY.md §2):** Gradience's Weyl-perturbation argument for compression safety is correct but imprecise — it bounds perturbation size without characterizing when the perturbation regime changes qualitatively. The intruder threshold marks precisely that regime change: below threshold, truncation is safe perturbation theory; above threshold, the update has restructured the representation.

- **Two-regime distinction (THEORY.md §2, lines 96–116):** Gradience already distinguishes compression of pretrained models (regime a) from compatibility assessment of fine-tuned adapters (regime b). The intruder threshold provides the formal criterion for this distinction: layers where the LoRA update exceeds the threshold are in regime (b) by construction.

- **Rank policy refinement:** The intruder threshold could serve as a sixth rank policy — a principled, RMT-derived partition point that doesn't require energy thresholds or knee detection heuristics.

### Integration priority: **CRITICAL**

This is the single most important external paper for Gradience since Tian et al. (2026). It provides the formal RMT machinery that Gradience's empirical program has been pointing toward but hasn't had. Recommended actions:
1. Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` §9 at HIGH priority
2. Compute the intruder threshold on Gradience's existing adapter corpus (N07, N127, N133 data) to check whether it predicts the same layers Gradience flags
3. Test whether the threshold correlates with W₀ energy concentration (the predictor that works) better than with W₀ spectral gap (the predictor that fails)
4. Consider implementing as a rank policy in `vnext/policy/`

## 2. Spectral Phase Transitions and Trainability in Neural Network Learning Dynamics

**Paper:** Chanju Park, Dario Bocchi, Francesco D'Amico, Biagio Lucini, Gert Aarts. arXiv:2606.28486 (June 2026)
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28486

### What it does

Models SGD as evolving a random matrix ensemble and derives a **phase diagram of trainability** as a function of learning rate and initial weight variance. The key mechanism is a BBP-type transition: during training, isolated eigenvalues detach from the random bulk of the weight spectrum, marking the onset of feature learning. The paper derives this analytically in a solvable linear teacher-student model and extends numerically to nonlinear and stochastic settings. Reports spectral alignment emerging during training in more realistic simulations.

### Why it matters for Gradience

This paper provides formal theoretical grounding for three of Gradience's core empirical observations:

- **Phase transition detection (THEORY.md §4, FINDINGS.md §§15–18):** Gradience's `research/phase_transitions.py` uses heuristic thresholds (autocorrelation time, variance ratio) borrowed from statistical mechanics. This paper derives the phase transition from first principles — the BBP transition IS the phase transition. If the BBP framework applies to the LoRA setting, it would replace Gradience's heuristic detection with a principled one.

- **Learning rate modulates spectral dynamics (FINDINGS.md §18):** Gradience found that high learning rate produces markedly lower DFA persistence (α ≈ 1.57 vs ≈ 1.90–2.07). The paper's phase diagram explicitly models trainability as a function of learning rate — high LR may push the system past the BBP transition into a qualitatively different spectral regime, explaining the DFA exponent separation.

- **Curvature telemetry lead-lag (FINDINGS.md §16a):** The paper's spectral alignment emerging during training is the theoretical counterpart of Gradience's empirical finding that Hessian energy leads validation accuracy by 3–6 updates. The BBP transition provides a candidate mechanism: isolated eigenvalues detaching from the bulk IS the curvature event that precedes performance improvement.

### Integration priority: **HIGH**

The formal framework is compelling and addresses Gradience's phase transition machinery directly. However, the gap between the solvable model and LoRA fine-tuning of large models is non-trivial — the linear teacher-student setup may not capture the complexities of the LoRA parameterization. Recommended actions:
1. Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` §9 at MEDIUM priority (replicate before integrating)
2. Check whether the BBP transition threshold can be computed from Gradience's existing curvature telemetry data (N07 Experiment B)
3. Test whether the predicted phase boundary (in learning-rate × variance space) matches the DFA exponent separation from Study 12

### Caveats
- Primary results are in a solvable linear model; nonlinear extensions are numerical, not analytical
- Multi-author team from lattice QCD / statistical mechanics — methodologically strong but may not fully account for LoRA-specific structure (low-rank parameterization, adapter initialization)
- Posted June 2026, no peer review yet

## 3. Predicting LLM Compression Degradation from Spectral Statistics

**Paper:** Mingxue (Mercy) Xu, Imperial College London. arXiv:2604.18085 (April 2026)
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18085

### What it does

Proposes using **stable rank × compression ratio** as a predictor of performance degradation under low-rank compression. Tests on Qwen3 and Gemma3 models under four compression methods (vanilla SVD, two ASVD variants, SVD-LLM). Achieves leave-one-out cross-validation Pearson correlations of **0.890 for attention layers** and **0.839 for MLP layers**. Proposes a predict-then-compress workflow: estimate degradation from weight-only spectral statistics before spending compute on compression and evaluation.

### Why it matters for Gradience

This paper provides independent, external validation of Gradience's core diagnostic framework:

- **Stable rank as the operative measure (THEORY.md §1):** Gradience uses stable rank as its primary effective-dimensionality metric. This paper independently arrives at stable rank as the best spectral predictor of compression outcomes — not entropy effective rank, not energy rank, but stable rank specifically. Two independent programs converging on the same metric is strong evidence for its construct validity.

- **Predict-then-compress is Gradience's workflow:** The paper's proposed workflow — measure spectral statistics first, then decide how much to compress — is architecturally identical to Gradience's audit-then-act pipeline. External validation of the workflow pattern itself.

- **Quantitative compression prediction (FINDINGS.md §1):** Gradience established that 50% compression is generally safe across 86 adapters. This paper provides a quantitative model for predicting per-layer degradation, which could refine Gradience's energy_threshold policy with a degradation-calibrated variant.

- **Attention vs. MLP differential (FINDINGS.md §5):** Gradience found the attention/MLP utilization gap vanishes at n=86 (0.167 vs 0.166). This paper reports different prediction accuracy for attention (0.890) vs MLP (0.839), suggesting the two module types may have different spectral-to-performance relationships even if their utilization is similar.

### Integration priority: **MODERATE-HIGH**

The paper validates Gradience's core metrics and workflow in a different application context (full model compression vs. LoRA adapter compression). The conceptual connection is strong but the application domains differ. Recommended actions:
1. Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` §9 at MEDIUM priority
2. Test whether the stable_rank × compression_ratio predictor applies to Gradience's LoRA adapter compression data (Study 17, bench suite)
3. Compare the paper's per-layer prediction accuracy with Gradience's rank policy disagreement patterns (FINDINGS.md §6)

### Caveats
- Validated only on Qwen3 and Gemma3 model families; cross-family generalization untested
- Full model compression, not LoRA adapter compression — the spectral statistics of W₀ and ΔW are different objects
- Solo-authored preprint, April 2026
