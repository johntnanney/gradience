### 1. Ahnouch & Elaachak (2026), *Spectral Rank Certification for Foundation Model Adapters* — CRITICAL candidate

**Authors / venue / date:** Mohammed Ahnouch, Lotfi Elaachak. KDD 2026 Workshop (TensorKDD). Submitted 2026-08-15.

**arXiv:** [arXiv:2608.15351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15351)

**One-sentence summary:** Develops a finite-sample statistical inference framework for effective rank structure in public LoRA adapters, distinguishing nominal rank (design parameter) from calibrated spectral evidence (inferential quantity) via chi-square divergence, BBP phase transitions, Monte Carlo p-values, and BH-corrected module-wise testing.

**Connection to program:**

This is the most directly aligned external paper to Gradience's core mission since the project began. Point-by-point:

1. **Same empirical question.** Gradience's core audit pipeline measures effective rank vs nominal rank via `energy_rank_90` (minimal k capturing 90% Frobenius energy). This paper formalizes the same question using random matrix theory — the Baik-Ben Arous-Péché (BBP) phase transition provides a principled signal/noise boundary, complementing Gradience's Gavish-Donoho/Marchenko-Pastur approach. The paper's "empirical-null workflow for PEFT LoRA adapters" is a more rigorous version of what `vnext/audit/lora_audit.py` does.

2. **Same empirical finding.** The paper audits 26 public adapters across 684 modules and 6 architecture families, finding calibrated effective rank is "typically much smaller than nominal rank and differs systematically from 95% energy retention." This directly confirms Gradience's Finding 1-3 (mean utilization 0.172 across 86 adapters, 22 architectures) from an independent group using independent methodology.

3. **Methodological upgrade path.** The chi-square divergence for rank-one reference experiments and the finite-sample Le Cam bound are formal statistical inference tools that Gradience currently lacks. Gradience uses heuristic thresholds (energy_rank_90, stable_rank); this paper provides calibrated p-values and multiple-testing correction (BH procedure). This could upgrade the QA Artifact's eligibility classification from heuristic to statistically grounded.

4. **Addresses open questions.** Directly relevant to THEORY.md open question #5 (generalization bounds from spectra — finite-sample bounds are provided) and tangentially to #3 (spectrum universality — the 31,770 public-checkpoint spectra rows are the largest spectral census available).

**Why CRITICAL candidate:** This paper is either the strongest external validation of Gradience's spectral audit framework or its strongest competitor, depending on positioning. The empirical-null methodology could be adopted as a complementary metric alongside `energy_rank_90`, or the two approaches could be benchmarked against each other. The 31,770-spectra dataset is also a potential replication/validation resource for Gradience's findings. For N134, this is convergent evidence that spectral rank certification is a recognized measurement problem — supports the measurement-discipline framing. The KDD Workshop venue is modest but the methodology is rigorous.

**Recommended action:** Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` Section 4 at **HIGH** tier (or potentially Section 2 MUST-CITE if the program decides to adopt the BBP-based certification as a complementary metric). Full-text review needed to assess: (a) whether the chi-square/BBP approach can be directly integrated into Gradience's audit pipeline as a second-opinion metric, (b) how the 95%-energy-retention baseline in the paper compares to Gradience's 90% threshold, (c) whether the 26-adapter audit overlaps with Gradience's 86-adapter census (potential for combined dataset). The 2026-04-03 public ecosystem spectral census plan (`2026-04-03-public-ecosystem-spectral-census-plan.md`) should be revisited in light of this paper's 31,770-row dataset.

### 2. Barazandeh (2026), *What Does the Rank Buy? A Spectral and Distributional Analysis of Low-Rank Adaptation* — HIGH

**Authors / venue / date:** Babak Barazandeh. Submitted September 2026.

**arXiv:** [arXiv:2609.32002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32002)

**One-sentence summary:** Proves that under per-factor norm budgets, LoRA's rank cap never binds — the linear-readout model class, its Rademacher complexity, and the distributional displacement are all rank-independent for r >= 1 — but that replacing per-factor budgets with a joint budget restores rank-sensitive complexity bounds where rank "sets the price of adaptation" by controlling the cost of canceling leading singular directions.

**Connection to program:**

This paper directly challenges and refines the theoretical assumptions underlying Gradience's rank policy framework:

1. **Rank-independence result.** Under per-factor norm budgets (the standard LoRA parameterization where A and B are independently norm-constrained), the paper proves that every complexity functional is maximized by a rank-one update. This means Gradience's rank suggestions and rank policies may be capturing a norm-budget interaction, not an intrinsic rank effect. The implication: when Gradience's audit finds that effective rank << nominal rank, this may reflect the optimizer's trajectory rather than a capacity constraint.

2. **Joint-budget restoration.** Under joint budgets (constraining the product BA rather than A and B separately), rank does matter: canceling the leading singular directions of the pretrained weight requires both sufficient rank and sufficient budget. This distinction is crucial for Gradience's spectral partitioning framework — the "price of adaptation" framing (cost of modifying high-SV directions) connects directly to Gradience's finding that the high-SV band is shared and the low-SV band is task-specific (FINDINGS.md sections 11-14).

3. **Theoretical implications for rank policies.** Gradience's rank policy analysis (`gradience/policy_analysis.py`, `vnext/policy/`) recommends rank allocations based on spectral measurements. This paper suggests those recommendations should be contextualized by the norm regime: rank recommendations are meaningful under joint budgets but potentially vacuous under per-factor budgets. This doesn't invalidate the audit (spectral measurements remain descriptive even if rank's causal role is budget-dependent) but it does refine the interpretation.

4. **Spectral connection.** The paper's core formalization — "rank sets the price of canceling leading singular directions" — is precisely the spectral-partitioning mechanism Gradience has empirically documented. The paper provides formal bounds on this mechanism.

**Why HIGH (not CRITICAL):** The paper is a theoretical contribution (proofs about complexity bounds) rather than an empirical challenge to Gradience's measurements. Gradience's spectral audit remains valid as a descriptive tool regardless of whether rank's effect on generalization is budget-dependent. However, the theoretical refinement is important for framing rank policy recommendations and for the N134 paper's claims about what spectral measurements measure. The paper is a single-author preprint (September 2026) without peer review yet; the venue and authorship pedigree need assessment at full-text review.

**Recommended action:** Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` Section 4 at **HIGH** tier. The rank-independence-under-per-factor-budgets result should be engaged in THEORY.md's open questions section (particularly #5, generalization bounds from spectra). Gradience's rank policy documentation (`docs/RANK_POLICIES.md`) should note the per-factor vs joint budget distinction as a theoretical caveat. The paper's formal "price of adaptation" framing may be the cleanest external formalization of Gradience's spectral partitioning intuition and could be cited in that context.

### 3. Wi & Park (2026), *Can Spectral-Clipping Enable Better Learning While Forgetting Less for Low-Rank Adaptation?* — HIGH

**Authors / venue / date:** Hyowon Wi, Noseong Park. **ACL 2026 Main Conference** (accepted). Submitted August 2026.

**arXiv / venue:** [arXiv:2608.12332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12332) · [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1179/)

**One-sentence summary:** Demonstrates that uncontrolled singular value growth in LoRA adapters causes catastrophic forgetting, and proposes SCLoRA — parameterized singular components with spectral clipping aware of the pretrained model's spectral distribution — achieving consistent improvements over LoRA variants while mitigating forgetting.

**Connection to program:**

1. **Confirms spectral partitioning.** The paper's core insight — principal singular components (large SVs) preserve pretrained knowledge while minor components (small SVs) require task-specific adaptation — is independently derived confirmation of Gradience's spectral partition finding (FINDINGS.md sections 11-14). Gradience showed empirically that the high-SV band is shared across tasks and the low-SV band is task-specific; this paper provides a complementary perspective from the forgetting/retention angle.

2. **Singular value dynamics.** The paper's analysis of uncontrolled SV growth during adaptation connects to Gradience's training dynamics telemetry (`vnext/telemetry.py`). The "spectral clipping" intervention is a spectral-distribution-aware regularization — Gradience's monitoring framework could detect the conditions that make clipping necessary (SV growth rate, spectral distribution shift).

3. **Forgetting as a spectral phenomenon.** The paper frames catastrophic forgetting as a spectral-domain pathology (uncontrolled SV growth), not a parameter-space one. This is the same register as Gradience's spectral monitoring — it suggests Gradience's audit could be extended to detect forgetting risk via SV growth rate analysis, complementing the existing merge-compatibility use case.

4. **High venue quality.** ACL 2026 Main Conference is a top-tier NLP venue, lending credibility to the spectral-analysis register that Gradience operates in. The paper's acceptance signals that the research community recognizes spectral properties of LoRA adapters as a productive analytical lens.

**Why HIGH (not CRITICAL):** While the paper confirms the spectral partition finding, it operates on a different application surface (forgetting mitigation during training, not merge compatibility assessment post-training). The method (SCLoRA) is an intervention, not a measurement tool, so it doesn't directly compete with or extend Gradience's audit pipeline. However, it validates the spectral-domain framework and provides a high-venue-quality citation anchor for Gradience's spectral partition claims. The connection to THEORY.md open question #7 (curvature-partition correspondence) is tangential but present: if SV growth tracks curvature changes, that's evidence for the Hessian-spectrum alignment hypothesis.

**Recommended action:** Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` Section 4 at **HIGH** tier. Cite as independent confirmation of spectral partitioning in any manuscript that discusses the high-SV/low-SV band structure. Evaluate whether Gradience's telemetry could incorporate SV growth rate monitoring as a forgetting-risk indicator (design doc candidate for `docs/design/`). The ACL 2026 venue makes this the highest-profile independent confirmation of the spectral-partition register.
