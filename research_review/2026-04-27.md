### Heineman, Hofmann, Magnusson, Gu, Smith, Hajishirzi, Lo, Dodge (2025), *Signal and Noise: A Framework for Reducing Uncertainty in Language Model Evaluation* — HIGH

**Authors / venue / date:** David Heineman, Valentin Hofmann, Ian Magnusson, Yuling Gu, Noah A. Smith, Hannaneh Hajishirzi, Kyle Lo, Jesse Dodge (Allen Institute for AI / University of Washington). arXiv preprint, **submitted 2025-08-18**, CC-BY 4.0 licensed; no journal venue stated yet. Companion repo: [allenai/signal-and-noise](https://github.com/allenai/signal-and-noise). Released dataset: 900K evaluation results, 200M instances.

**arXiv / DOI:** [arXiv:2508.13144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13144) · [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2508.13144](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2508.13144) · [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.13144v1) · [GitHub](https://github.com/allenai/signal-and-noise)

**One-sentence summary:** Defines a benchmark's "signal" as its ability to separate better from worse models and "noise" as its sensitivity to random training-step variability, demonstrates that high signal-to-noise predicts reliability of small-scale decisions and lower scaling-law prediction error, and tests three interventions — switching metric (e.g., perplexity over accuracy), filtering noisy subtasks, and averaging intermediate checkpoints — across 30 benchmarks and 375 open-weight models from 60M to 32B parameters.

**Connection to program:** Direct conceptual overlap with the benchmark reliability study's **H2 generalizability hypothesis**. The "signal" quantity is a ratio of systematic between-model variance to total variance — mathematically the same family of object as the benchmark study's generalizability coefficient under the G-theory decomposition (§7.2 Analysis 2). The "noise" quantity is a within-checkpoint-trajectory variability measure — adjacent to but distinct from the benchmark study's measurement-condition variance (§7.1 Analysis 1), which decomposes across prompt × seed × scoring-rule rather than training-step. The 30 × 375 empirical scope is much larger than the benchmark study's 5 × 3 primary panel and is on partially overlapping benchmarks (the paper uses the same constrained-choice family — ARC, HellaSwag, MMLU, etc.). The author overlap with Brittlebench (Dodge) and the Allen AI lineage (Magnusson, Hofmann, Gu, Lo, Hajishirzi) makes engagement non-optional for the benchmark study's manuscript: a reviewer familiar with this paper will expect the benchmark study to position against it.

**Why this criticality tier:** HIGH, not CRITICAL. The benchmark reliability study's distinctive prescriptive contribution — the **decimal-place precision tolerance schedule** under §3.5 — is not formulated in Signal and Noise, which produces intervention recommendations (switch to perplexity, filter noisy subtasks, average checkpoints) rather than reporting-precision licensing. Empirical-scope non-overlap is partial but informative: Signal and Noise's noise variable is training-step-trajectory variability (relevant to small-scale-decision-making during training) whereas the benchmark study's measurement-condition variance is prompt × seed × scoring-rule (relevant to evaluation reporting). The two are complementary and address different decision contexts: signal-and-noise informs *how to design or select benchmarks for training-decision use*; the benchmark study informs *how to report benchmark accuracy after training is complete*. The benchmark study's manuscript will need to engage Signal and Noise explicitly in §2.1 (related work) and §7.2 (generalizability discussion) — the "signal" framing is the field-recognized vocabulary that the G-theory generalizability coefficient maps onto, and not citing the AI2/Dodge corpus would be a citation-completeness gap. **Not CRITICAL because** neither the protocol nor the contribution claim is supplanted; the tolerance schedule remains the distinctive prescriptive output. **HIGH for benchmark study; LOW for N134** (cite-as-evidence-of-broader-program-convergence at most, in the parallel-development paragraph).

**Recommended action:** Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` Section 4 — Tracked parallel work — at **HIGH** tier. Add to manuscript citation list for the benchmark reliability study (introduction §2.1, related work; §7.2 generalizability discussion). Flag for the manuscript's positioning paragraph as the fifth voice in the parallel-development register Section 8 has been tracking (Messing 2026 + Camuffo et al. 2026 + NIST 800-2/800-3 + NeurIPS 2025 construct-validity review + Brittlebench + **Signal and Noise**). The convergent diagnosis — single-occasion benchmark accuracy reporting is under-specified — is now field-wide; the benchmark study's distinctive contribution (decimal-place precision licensing) remains unsupplanted. Closes the 2026-04-26 note-for-next-reviewer about coverage discipline: an August 2025 paper from this author group should have surfaced in the inaugural pass, and acknowledgment that it didn't is part of the audit trail. **No protocol implications** at v1.1.2.

**Framework-applied note:** The paper's signal and noise estimates are reported as point statistics on 30 benchmarks; the abstract does not describe whether per-benchmark signal-noise estimates are accompanied by reliability-across-model-architecture confidence bounds, nor whether the 2-3pp "predicts full-evaluation performance within 2-3 percentage points" is a per-cell or aggregate statistic. Full-text review at manuscript-citation-staging time would clarify whether the methodology is itself measurement-disciplined in the framework's register, or whether it presents the same gap the framework critiques in §1.2 of N134 (point estimates without explicit reliability infrastructure). The recursive framework-applied opportunity is genuine: the benchmark study could engage Signal and Noise at exactly the level Brittlebench was engaged in yesterday's report — comparing what signal/noise discipline reports vs. what tolerance-schedule discipline reports, on overlapping substrates.

**Recommended action:** Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` Section 4 — Tracked parallel work — at **HIGH** tier with note that it is the canonical recent philosophy-of-ML reference for construct-validity-in-LLM-research. **Framework-applied note:** Single-author philosophy paper; no quantitative claims to subject to recursive measurement-discipline scrutiny.
