### 1. Feng, Lin & Kao (May 2026), *Learning in the Fisher Subspace: A Guided Initialization for LoRA Fine-Tuning* — HIGH

**Authors / venue / date:** Zhi-Quan Feng, Ying-Jia Lin, Hung-Yu Kao. arXiv preprint, submitted 2026-05-01.

**arXiv:** [arXiv:2605.01046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01046) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.01046)

**One-sentence summary:** Proposes a Fisher-information-guided framework for LoRA initialization that selects adaptation subspaces based on data-aware sensitivity (Fisher curvature) rather than weight-only magnitude, demonstrating consistent and significant downstream performance improvements across diverse tasks and modalities.

**Connection to program:** This paper directly addresses **Open Problem #6 in THEORY.md** — the Hessian-spectrum alignment question: "Formal proof that top-k singular directions of Delta_W approximate top-k eigendirections of the task Hessian restricted to the LoRA subspace." Feng et al. provide empirical evidence for exactly this correspondence by showing that Fisher-guided subspace selection (which targets the highest-curvature directions of the loss landscape) outperforms SVD-of-weight-based initialization. The key finding — that data-aware sensitivity governs which subspace is optimal, not weight magnitude — is directly testable within Gradience's existing research instruments: the `gradience.research.fisher` module already computes empirical Fisher spectra and natural gradient alignment, and the `gradience.research.spectral_extended` module tracks rank dynamics. A comparison of Fisher-guided vs. weight-SVD subspace selection using Gradience's telemetry infrastructure would directly address the open problem.

Additionally relevant to THEORY.md §4.2 (Information-Geometric Perspective): the paper's empirical results bear on the open question "Does erank(Delta_W) predict erank(F) restricted to the adapter subspace?" — if Fisher-guided initialization outperforms weight-SVD initialization, it implies the two eigenspectra are not perfectly aligned, which is informative for the program's theoretical claims about the Fisher-spectrum correspondence.

**Why this criticality tier:** HIGH. Addresses a genuinely open problem named in THEORY.md with new empirical methodology the program lacked. The `gradience.research.fisher` module was designed to investigate exactly this type of correspondence. Not CRITICAL because: (a) the merge-substrate line is negatively settled and this paper is about initialization rather than merge diagnostics; (b) the program's current active execution path (benchmark reliability, substrate generalization) is not directly affected; (c) the paper's empirical scope (initialization improvement) is narrower than the program's theoretical question (full correspondence proof). HIGH because the empirical evidence is directly relevant to the program's theoretical framework and could inform a future paper on the Fisher-spectrum correspondence.

**Recommended action:** Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` Section 4 at HIGH tier. Stage as a primary reference for any future work on Open Problem #6. The paper's methodology (Fisher-guided subspace selection) is a candidate replication target using Gradience's existing research instruments. If the program ever activates the Fisher-spectrum correspondence line, this paper provides the concrete empirical anchor the line has been missing.

**Framework-applied note:** The paper reports "consistent and significant improvements" — full-text review should check whether improvements are reported with per-seed confidence intervals and whether the comparison baseline (weight-SVD initialization) is the specific formulation Gradience uses (energy_rank_90-based truncation) or a different SVD variant.

### 2. Lee, Jeong & Yoon (March 2026), *Label-Free Cross-Task LoRA Merging with Null-Space Compression* — HIGH

**Authors / venue / date:** Wonyoung Lee, Wooseong Jeong, Kuk-Jin Yoon (KAIST). arXiv preprint, submitted 2026-03-27.

**arXiv:** [arXiv:2603.26317](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26317) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.26317) · [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26317v1)

**One-sentence summary:** Introduces Null-Space Compression (NSC) Merging, a label-free, output-agnostic method that derives merge weights from the geometric compression of the LoRA down-projection factor A's null space during fine-tuning — a signal that correlates with performance and generalizes across classification, regression, and sequence generation.

**Connection to program:** This paper introduces a new geometric observable — the null-space ratio of the A matrix — that is directly computable from existing Gradience spectral measurements and has significant implications for the merge pipeline:

1. **Merge pipeline enrichment.** Gradience's merge analysis (`vnext/merge/`) currently uses principal angles between adapter subspaces (Bjorck-Golub method on U_a^T U_b) and spectral partitioning (high-SV vs low-SV band alignment). The null-space compression ratio is a complementary signal: it measures how much of the A matrix's capacity was consumed during training, which the paper shows correlates with adapter quality. This could inform `PairDiagnosis` and `MergeRecommendation` without requiring any evaluation data.

2. **Cross-task generalization.** A key limitation of Gradience's validated merge framework is its encoder-scale, same-task empirical base. NSC explicitly demonstrates cross-task merging (classification + regression + generation) on both vision and NLP — exactly the substrate-generalization direction the program has committed to as option (a). The paper's observation that entropy-based surrogate signals (used by some prior methods) fail on regression tasks is relevant to Gradience's own spectral entropy metrics.

3. **A-matrix specificity.** THEORY.md and the merge pipeline treat Delta_W = BA as a single object for SVD analysis. NSC's finding that the A (down-projection) factor's null-space geometry is independently informative suggests that per-factor analysis (A vs B separately) may carry diagnostic signal the current pipeline misses. This connects to Tang et al. (2026) "Crowded in B-Space" (already tracked at HIGH), which found A/B asymmetry in shared-direction accumulation — the two papers together point to a systematic A/B decomposition the program should consider.

**Why this criticality tier:** HIGH. The null-space ratio is a novel geometric observable directly computable from Gradience's existing SVD infrastructure that could enrich the merge diagnostics. The cross-task empirical scope addresses the program's substrate-generalization direction. Not CRITICAL because: (a) the merge-substrate line is negatively settled for current execution; (b) the paper's prescriptive output (merge weights) is a method contribution, not a theoretical insight that changes the program's framework; (c) the paper is March 2026 and was not caught by the April daily reviews, suggesting it may be on the boundary of the program's attention scope. HIGH because of the geometric novelty (null-space ratio as diagnostic) and the cross-task empirical base that the program's option (a) direction needs.

**Recommended action:** Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` Section 4 at HIGH tier alongside Tang et al. (2026) "Crowded in B-Space." The two papers together motivate a per-factor (A vs B) spectral decomposition study that the program has not yet conducted. If option (a) substrate generalization activates, NSC's cross-task results on vision + NLP provide an empirical anchor for heterogeneous-task merge validation. The null-space ratio is implementable within the existing `vnext/audit/lora_audit.py` framework as a low-cost addition to the spectral measurement suite.

**Framework-applied note:** The paper's key claim — null-space ratio correlates with performance — is demonstrated across 20 vision tasks and 6 NLI benchmarks, but the correlation evidence should be reviewed for per-task reliability. A framework-applied question: is the null-space ratio reliable across seeds for the same (task, architecture) pair? If it has low cross-seed ICC, the diagnostic signal is noisy.

### 3. Rushka & Klabjan (February 2026), *Rank-Accuracy Trade-off for LoRA: A Gradient-Flow Analysis* — MEDIUM

**Authors / venue / date:** Michael Rushka, Diego Klabjan. arXiv preprint, submitted 2026-02-14.

**arXiv:** [arXiv:2602.10212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10212) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.10212)

**One-sentence summary:** Derives closed-form analytical relationships between LoRA rank and accuracy via gradient flow analysis, establishing explicit rank-accuracy trade-off formulas for trace-squared and Frobenius-norm low-rank approximation loss functions, and proving that gradient flow dynamics are identical for simultaneous and sequential LoRA parameter updates.

**Connection to program:** This paper addresses **Open Problem #4 in THEORY.md** — spectral scaling laws: "How does entropy effective rank scale with model size, dataset size, and compute?" While Rushka & Klabjan's analysis is narrower (rank vs accuracy under specific loss functions rather than the full (model_size, dataset_size, compute) scaling), the closed-form relationships they derive provide the first rigorous analytical framework for understanding why rank-1 LoRA sometimes matches full fine-tuning — a phenomenon the program has observed empirically (mean utilization 0.172 across 86 adapters in the public census) but has not been able to explain theoretically.

The gradient-flow perspective is also relevant to THEORY.md §3 (Training Dynamics): the proof that gradient flow equations are identical for simultaneous vs. sequential LoRA updates settles a question about training dynamics that was implicit in the program's telemetry analysis. The dynamical systems formulation may provide a theoretical backbone for the rank evolution tracking in `gradience.research.spectral_extended`.

**Why this criticality tier:** MEDIUM, not HIGH. The analysis is under simplified loss functions (trace-squared and Frobenius-norm) that are distant from the cross-entropy losses used in actual LoRA fine-tuning. The closed-form results are therefore illustrative rather than directly applicable to the program's empirical setting. The gradient-flow perspective is theoretically clean but may not survive the transition to stochastic gradient descent with realistic loss landscapes. MEDIUM because: (a) it addresses an open problem but under simplified conditions; (b) the rank-accuracy trade-off formulas could inform the rank suggestion policy (`vnext/policy/`) if the program wanted to provide theoretical justification for its rank recommendations; (c) the simultaneous-vs-sequential equivalence result is a useful theoretical anchor.

**Recommended action:** Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` Section 4 at MEDIUM tier. Track as a theoretical reference for the spectral-scaling-laws open problem. If the program ever produces a theory paper on rank dynamics, this is a citation-and-extend target: the gradient-flow analysis under simplified losses provides the starting point, and the program's empirical evidence (86-adapter census, telemetry-tracked rank evolution) provides the data to test whether the closed-form predictions hold under realistic conditions. No action-forcing implications for current execution.
