### 1. SpectralLoRA — Singh (April 2026)

**Full title**: *SpectralLoRA: Is Low-Frequency Structure Sufficient for LoRA Adaptation? A Spectral Analysis of Weight Updates*
**arXiv**: [2604.10649](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10649)
**Submitted**: 2026-04-12

**Summary**: Systematic empirical study of the spectral structure of LoRA weight updates using 2D Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) analysis across BERT-base and RoBERTa-base on four GLUE benchmarks (SST-2, MNLI, CoLA, QQP). Key findings: (a) LoRA updates are universally dominated by low-frequency components — just 33% of DCT coefficients capture 90% of total spectral energy; (b) retaining only 10% of frequency coefficients reduces adapter storage by 10× while sacrificing only 1.95pp on SST-2; (c) frequency masking at k=50% *improves* over full LoRA on 3 of 8 model–task pairs, suggesting high-frequency components act as adaptation noise; (d) RoBERTa-base is systematically more spectrally compressible than BERT-base across all tasks; (e) task complexity governs spectral sensitivity (NLI tasks require more frequency budget than sentiment classification). Uses SVD–DCT correlation analysis to connect the DCT empirical constant to the spectral dynamics of SGD.

**Relevance to Gradience**: This paper studies the *same measurement object* as Gradience (spectral structure of LoRA weight updates) but through a complementary lens (frequency-domain DCT vs. Gradience's singular-value-domain SVD). The "33% of coefficients capture 90% of energy" finding is a frequency-domain analog of Gradience's "mean utilization 0.172" finding (FINDINGS.md §1–3) — both demonstrate that LoRA adapters are massively over-parameterized, but in different spectral bases. The SVD–DCT correlation analysis in the paper could provide a formal bridge between DCT compressibility and Gradience's energy-rank metrics. The task-complexity–spectral-sensitivity finding parallels Gradience's task-family-aware configuration validation (`vnext/policy/check.py`). The architecture-dependent compressibility (RoBERTa > BERT) is a cross-architecture result that could inform Gradience's spectral scaling law open question (THEORY.md §7.2.4).

**Recommended tier**: **HIGH**. Same measurement object, complementary methodology. The DCT–SVD bridge could strengthen Gradience's theoretical framework and provide an independent validation pathway for the compression-potential findings. Should be engaged in any future revision of FINDINGS.md or THEORY.md spectral-analysis sections. Not MUST-CITE because the DCT basis is distinct from the SVD basis the program operates in, and the encoder-scale (BERT/RoBERTa) scope does not overlap with Gradience's primary decoder-scale (Mistral-7B) regime.

### 2. Distributional Spectral Diagnostics for Localizing Grokking Transitions — Wang, Ying, Kanamori (May 2026)

**Full title**: *Distributional Spectral Diagnostics for Localizing Grokking Transitions*
**arXiv**: [2605.08237](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08237)
**Submitted**: 2026-05-07
**Authors**: Ziyue Wang, Yufeng Ying, Takafumi Kanamori (Institute of Science Tokyo; University of Science and Technology of China)

**Summary**: Formulates grokking transition localization as a formal diagnostic problem with explicit threshold/FPR/lead-time trade-off. Task-dependent observables are summarized as empirical distributions, mapped to Wasserstein/quantile coordinates, and analyzed by Hankel dynamic mode decomposition (DMD); the resulting reconstruction residual, together with spectrum and effective rank, forms the diagnostic output. On held-out modular-addition Transformer runs, the residual achieves AUROC ≈ 0.93 for grokking-vs-non-grokking discrimination at the run level; under a fixed sustained-threshold operating rule, true-positive alarms precede generalization onset with lead time reported jointly with false-alarm rate and uncertainty intervals.

**Relevance to Gradience**: This is the most methodologically aligned external paper to Gradience's phase transition detection program that has surfaced since the Spectral Edge Thesis (Xu 2026, already MUST-CITE). Three specific connections:

1. **Effective rank as diagnostic variable**: The paper uses effective rank as one of its core diagnostic outputs — the same metric that Gradience's `PhaseTransitionTracker` and `GrokDetector` track. The paper's FINDING_phase_transition.txt documents a +11% effective-rank jump coinciding with generalization onset; this paper formalizes the detection of exactly that kind of transition with proper receiver-operating-characteristic analysis.

2. **Measurement-discipline alignment**: The paper frames detection quality in terms of AUROC, false-positive rate, and lead time with uncertainty intervals — exactly the kind of measurement-discipline framing that Gradience's N134 manuscript argues for. This is a worked example of treating a training-dynamics diagnostic as a measurement instrument with operating characteristics, rather than an ad-hoc heuristic.

3. **Complementary detection methodology**: Gradience's `PhaseTransitionTracker` uses integrated autocorrelation time, variance ratios, and stationarity tests. This paper's Hankel DMD approach is a fundamentally different detection strategy that could serve as an independent validation pathway or be integrated as an alternative detector in `gradience/research/phase_transitions.py`.

**Recommended tier**: **HIGH**. The measurement-discipline framing (AUROC, FPR, lead-time trade-off) is directly aligned with the N134 manuscript's thesis. The effective-rank-as-diagnostic overlap is striking. Strongest candidate for integration into Gradience's phase transition detection framework. Cite in any future revision to THEORY.md §6 (Phase Transitions) and as an external validation of the "effective rank as order parameter" finding. Not MUST-CITE because (a) the program's phase-transition work is deferred per the options memo for current execution, and (b) the paper's scope (modular-addition Transformers) does not overlap with LoRA fine-tuning dynamics — though the methodology transfers directly.

### 3. Weight Decay Regimes in Grokking Transformers: Cheap Online Diagnostics — Verma (May 2026)

**Full title**: *Weight Decay Regimes in Grokking Transformers: Cheap Online Diagnostics*
**arXiv**: [2605.20441](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20441)
**Submitted**: 2026-05-19
**Author**: Lucky Verma (Independent Researcher)
**Resources**: [GitHub](https://github.com/lucky-verma/grokking-diagnostics), [HuggingFace dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lucky-verma/grokking-diagnostics-runs)

**Summary**: Across eleven experimental conditions and three model scales (0.82M to 85M parameters), demonstrates that weight decay acts as a scalar empirical control parameter separating memorization, developmental grokking, and collapse regimes in Transformers trained on modular arithmetic. Introduces two cheap online diagnostics — mean pairwise attention-head cosine similarity and entropy standard deviation — that track training dynamics from attention activations alone and complement loss-landscape diagnostics at lower compute cost. A near-transition logistic fit localizes the memorization-to-developmental boundary at λ_c = 0.0158 (95% CI [0.0109, 0.0200], N=210); a power-law fit gives empirical exponent ν = 0.757 (CI [0.725, 0.799]).

**Relevance to Gradience**: Three connections to the Gradience program:

1. **Cheap online diagnostics for phase transitions**: Gradience's current phase transition detection (`PhaseTransitionTracker`) relies on effective-rank and autocorrelation-time trajectories computed from weight-update SVD — moderately expensive. The attention-head cosine similarity and entropy standard deviation diagnostics proposed here are orders of magnitude cheaper (computed from forward-pass activations, not weight SVD). If these diagnostics transfer to LoRA fine-tuning (an open question), they could be implemented as lightweight probes in `gradience/finetune/` or `gradience/vnext/integrations/` alongside the existing `GradienceCallback`.

2. **Weight decay as control parameter**: Gradience's policy/config validation (`vnext/policy/check.py`) already checks weight decay ranges per task family. This paper provides a quantitative phase diagram with a localized critical boundary (λ_c = 0.0158 with CIs) that could inform the policy engine's weight-decay thresholds — shifting from heuristic bounds to empirically calibrated regime boundaries.

3. **Grokking experiment cross-validation**: Gradience has six grokking experiment directories (`experiments/grokking*`, `experiments/transformer_grok`, `experiments/transformer_multiseed`). This paper's 11-condition × 3-scale design and public HuggingFace dataset provide a cross-validation target for Gradience's grokking findings (architecture-dependent grokking, effective-rank-as-order-parameter).

**Recommended tier**: **MEDIUM-HIGH** (between MEDIUM and HIGH). The cheap online diagnostics are methodologically novel and practically valuable. The quantitative phase boundary with confidence intervals is well-aligned with the measurement-discipline stance. Tier below HIGH because: (a) the phase-transition research line is deferred for current execution; (b) single-author independent researcher — epistemic weight one tier below peer-reviewed venue; (c) transfer to LoRA fine-tuning dynamics is untested. However, if the program reactivates the phase-transition research line, this becomes HIGH immediately. The public dataset and code make it a strong candidate for reanalysis with Gradience's spectral tools.
