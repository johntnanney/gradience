### 1. Lu, Bhaskar, Axelrod, Tolstaya, Emrich (2026), *Model Merging on Loss Landscape: A Geometry Perspective* — HIGH

**Authors / venue / date:** Juanwu Lu, Anand Bhaskar, Brian Axelrod, Ekaterina Tolstaya, Tristan Emrich (Purdue University / Waymo LLC). arXiv preprint, **submitted 2026-05-26**.

**arXiv:** [2605.26693](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26693)

**One-sentence summary:** Casts model merging as computing the Fréchet mean on a Riemannian manifold restricted to the low-rank subspace spanned by task vectors, decomposes the merging error bound into *subspace Fréchet variance* and *residual energy*, and proves closed-form conditions under which curvature-aware merging outperforms flat-geometry methods — unifying both curvature-aware and spectral merge methods as special cases of a single geometric framework.

**Connection to Gradience:**

This paper addresses the theoretical gap between Gradience's spectral triage (which operates on weight-space SVD geometry) and curvature-aware merge methods (which operate on loss-landscape Hessian geometry). The key contributions map directly onto Gradience's architecture:

1. **Error decomposition.** The decomposition of merging error into *subspace Fréchet variance* (how much task vectors disagree within the merge subspace) and *residual energy* (how much task-relevant information lies outside the merge subspace) provides a formal framework for Gradience's two-factor model. The subspace Fréchet variance term corresponds to what Gradience measures as subspace overlap and directional agreement; the residual energy term corresponds to the spectral tail that the tail-interference probe (CHG-005, THEORY.md §7.2) probed empirically.

2. **Unification of spectral and curvature methods.** The paper shows that flat-geometry merging (simple averaging, TIES, DARE) and curvature-aware merging are both special cases of the subspace Fréchet mean under different metric choices. This is structurally relevant to the THEORY.md §5.3 vision of a "unified spectral measurement framework" — Lu et al. provide a candidate mathematical formalism for the unification that Gradience's §5.3 describes qualitatively.

3. **Closed-form characterization of when curvature matters.** The conditions under which curvature-aware merging provably outperforms flat merging could inform Gradience's strategy recommendation pipeline. Currently, `MergeRecommendation.recommended_strategy` selects between `"linear"`, `"norm_equalized"`, and `"audit_aware"` based on empirical heuristics; Lu et al.'s theoretical conditions could provide principled thresholds.

4. **Low-rank subspace restriction.** The restriction to the subspace spanned by task vectors is the same operational regime Gradience's merge-audit pipeline works in — per-layer analysis of the LoRA update subspace. The Fréchet mean formulation provides a geometric interpretation of what the merge-audit pipeline is measuring: how well-conditioned the Fréchet mean problem is in the adapter subspace.

**Why this criticality tier:** HIGH. The paper provides the first formal framework that unifies the two measurement regimes Gradience currently straddles (weight-space SVD and loss-surface Hessian). It does not supplant Gradience's operational triage — EpiMer is an *execution* method (better merging), while Gradience is a *triage* method (deciding whether to merge) — but it provides the theoretical scaffolding that could formalize the triage decision. The error decomposition directly addresses the "structural-behavioral separation" finding (FINDINGS.md §9) by partitioning merge error into a structural component (Fréchet variance in the subspace) and a residual component (energy outside it). **Not CRITICAL** because: (a) very recent preprint (May 26, 2026), no peer review; (b) the empirical scope is unknown without full-text review; (c) the triage vs. execution distinction means Gradience's unique contribution (deciding *whether* to merge) is not directly addressed.

**Recommended action:** Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` Section 9 at **HIGH — replicate before integrating**. The natural test: take the existing Gradience validation corpus (encoder pairs with known merge outcomes) and check whether Lu et al.'s subspace Fréchet variance correlates with Gradience's compatibility score and pair risk labels. If it does, the Fréchet variance formulation provides a principled replacement for the current empirical thresholds. Also cite in THEORY.md §5.3 and §7.2 (concentration-weighted convergence bound) as a candidate formal framework for the unification agenda.

**Criticality rating: 7/10** — High theoretical relevance; could reshape the formal framework but does not invalidate existing empirical findings.

### 2. Zhang et al. (2025), *The Primacy of Magnitude in Low-Rank Adaptation* — HIGH

**Authors / venue / date:** Zicheng Zhang and collaborators. **NeurIPS 2025** (spotlight presentation). arXiv preprint: [2507.06558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06558), July 2025.

**One-sentence summary:** Proves that SVD-based spectral initialization for LoRA succeeds primarily via *magnitude amplification* — the boost in weight update magnitude — rather than through knowledge alignment in the spectral domain; proposes LoRAM, a magnitude-driven initialization that matches spectral initialization performance without SVD computation.

**Connection to Gradience:**

This paper poses the most direct challenge to Gradience's theoretical interpretation among all papers surveyed. The core claim — that magnitude, not spectral geometry, is the operative mechanism — touches the foundations of why spectral audit should work at all.

1. **Challenge to the spectral-geometry interpretation.** Gradience's entire triage framework rests on the premise that the SVD of LoRA weight updates reveals *geometric* structure (subspace orientations, angular relationships) that predicts merge compatibility. Zhang et al. argue that in the *initialization* setting, what appears to be a spectral-geometry effect is actually a magnitude effect. The question for Gradience is: does this magnitude-primacy finding extend from initialization to post-training audit? If the spectral profile of a trained adapter primarily reflects magnitude dynamics rather than geometric structure, then metrics like `mean_overlap` and `directional_agreement` may be measuring magnitude confounds rather than true subspace relationships.

2. **Implications for the spectral partitioning model.** The N127 spectral partitioning results (FINDINGS.md §§11–14) interpret the high-SV/low-SV alignment ratio as evidence that high-energy directions encode shared task structure. Zhang et al.'s framework suggests an alternative interpretation: high-SV directions are simply the directions with the largest magnitude updates, and their inter-adapter alignment could be a consequence of magnitude-driven convergence (all optimizers amplify the same large-magnitude directions) rather than task-geometric convergence. This alternative hypothesis is testable: if the alignment effect survives magnitude normalization (dividing out singular values before computing cosines), the geometric interpretation stands. If it disappears, the magnitude interpretation may be correct.

3. **Implications for merge triage.** The merge interaction term $z = \text{sign}(\delta) \cdot \cos(\theta) \cdot \cos(\phi)$ (Technical Report §2.3) is weighted by singular value magnitudes. If magnitude is primary, then the weighting may be the operative signal, not the angular terms — and merge compatibility would reduce to a magnitude-balance question (already captured by Gradience's `frobenius_ratio` and `magnitude_ratio` metrics) rather than a subspace-geometry question. This would dramatically simplify the triage model but also narrow its explanatory scope.

4. **Important scope limitation.** Zhang et al.'s analysis is about *initialization*, not about *post-training structure*. The question of whether magnitude primacy transfers from initialization dynamics to the spectral profile of a fully trained adapter is open. Gradience's validation corpus contains adapters that have been trained to convergence; the spectral structure at convergence may be governed by different principles than the spectral structure at initialization. Nonetheless, the challenge is real: any claim about spectral-geometry diagnostics must now explicitly engage the magnitude-primacy alternative.

**Why this criticality tier:** HIGH, approaching CRITICAL for the theoretical framework. A NeurIPS 2025 spotlight paper carries strong evidential weight, and the claim directly threatens the interpretive framework (though not the operational pipeline, which works empirically regardless of why). **Not CRITICAL because:** (a) the scope is initialization, not post-training audit; (b) Gradience's empirical validation (r = 0.846 dominance prediction, zero false positives on task-boundary detection, DeBERTa replication) stands regardless of interpretation; (c) the magnitude-normalization test described above is a tractable empirical check that could resolve the ambiguity. **HIGH because** it forces the program to either explicitly defend the geometric interpretation against the magnitude alternative or acknowledge magnitude as a confound in the spectral audit metrics.

**Recommended action:** Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` Section 9 at **HIGH — engage before next manuscript revision**. Two concrete actions: (1) Run the magnitude-normalization test on the existing validation corpus: compute inter-adapter alignment with and without SV weighting, and check whether the task-dependent partitioning (same-task 7.8× vs cross-task 2.5×) survives normalization. This is a single-afternoon script on existing data. (2) Cite-and-engage in THEORY.md §6 (spectral partitioning) and Technical Report §2.3 (interaction term) — the paper must be acknowledged as a serious alternative interpretation, with explicit statement of the empirical test that distinguishes the two accounts. Also note in THEORY.md §1 that the "magnitude vs. geometry" question is now an explicitly live debate rather than a background assumption.

**Criticality rating: 8/10** — Highest urgency of any paper in this review. The magnitude-primacy challenge could change the interpretive framework if not empirically resolved. Operationally non-threatening (the pipeline works regardless), but theoretically load-bearing.

### 3. Ledent, Alves, Lei (2025), *Generalization Bounds for Rank-sparse Neural Networks* — MEDIUM

**Authors / venue / date:** Antoine Ledent, Rodrigo Alves, Yunwen Lei. **NeurIPS 2025** (accepted). arXiv preprint: [2510.21945](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.21945), October 2025.

**One-sentence summary:** Proves generalization bounds for neural networks that exploit approximate low-rank structure of weight matrices via Schatten-p quasi-norms, achieving sample complexity $\tilde{O}(WrL^2)$ where $W$ and $L$ are width and depth and $r$ is the rank — the first bounds that tighten as the effective rank decreases.

**Connection to Gradience:**

This paper directly addresses one of the explicitly identified open theoretical questions in Gradience's research program — "Generalization bounds from spectra" (THEORY.md §7.2 and ROADMAP "Long-Term: Open Theoretical Questions").

1. **Direct answer to the open question.** THEORY.md §7.2 asks: "Can spectral properties of ΔW provide tighter generalization bounds than parameter-count-based bounds?" and notes that "PAC-Bayes bounds using spectral norms exist in the literature (Neyshabur et al., 2018; Arora et al., 2018) but have not been specialized to the low-rank LoRA parameterization where the spectral structure is particularly clean." Ledent et al. provide exactly this: bounds that tighten with rank, using the Schatten-p framework that naturally interpolates between rank-based and norm-based complexity measures.

2. **Connection to stable rank and energy rank.** The Schatten-p quasi-norm framework naturally includes stable rank as a special case. For p=2, the Schatten-2 norm is the Frobenius norm; the ratio $\|M\|_F^2 / \|M\|_2^2$ is exactly the stable rank that Gradience reports. For smaller p, the quasi-norms are more sensitive to rank sparsity, which maps onto what Gradience's `energy_rank_90` captures: the number of directions that carry meaningful energy. The bounds therefore provide theoretical justification for Gradience's core metrics: adapters with low stable rank (high spectral concentration) have provably smaller generalization gaps.

3. **Bottleneck rank observation.** The paper establishes the "bottleneck rank property" — that for gradient-trained networks, activations and weights converge to an approximately low-rank structure with a fixed bottleneck rank. This is an independent theoretical prediction of the same empirical phenomenon Gradience observes: LoRA adapters trained at rank 16 or 64 converge to effective rank 1.2–3.0 (FINDINGS.md §§1–3, §23). The convergence to a bottleneck rank is not just an empirical regularity but a theoretically predicted consequence of gradient-based training.

4. **Practical implications for compression safety.** If the generalization bound tightens with rank, then truncating to the effective rank (as Gradience's compression pipeline does) not only preserves spectral energy but also does not degrade the generalization guarantee. This provides a second theoretical argument for compression safety, complementing the Weyl perturbation bound in THEORY.md §2.

**Why this criticality tier:** MEDIUM, not HIGH. The paper answers an important open theoretical question but does not change the operational pipeline or challenge existing empirical findings. The bounds are proved for general neural networks, not specifically for LoRA; specializing to the LoRA parameterization (where the rank structure is explicit and clean) would require additional derivation. **Not HIGH because:** (a) the bounds are theoretical and not yet specialized to the LoRA setting; (b) no direct operational consequence — Gradience's compression and triage decisions are currently driven by empirical thresholds, and the theoretical bounds do not change those thresholds; (c) the bottleneck rank observation confirms rather than challenges existing findings. **MEDIUM because:** (a) it answers an explicit open question in the theory document; (b) a NeurIPS 2025 paper provides strong evidential weight; (c) the Schatten-p framework is the natural formal language for expressing Gradience's spectral metrics; (d) it strengthens the theoretical case for the program.

**Recommended action:** Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` Section 9 at **MEDIUM — cite and integrate into theoretical framework**. Cite in THEORY.md §7.2 ("Generalization bounds from spectra") as a partial resolution of the open question, with note that specialization to the LoRA parameterization is the remaining step. Cite in THEORY.md §2 (compression safety) as an independent theoretical argument complementing Weyl. Consider adding a future-work note about deriving LoRA-specific bounds using the Schatten-p framework applied to the product BA, which would give bounds in terms of exactly the metrics Gradience already computes. **No protocol implications.**

**Criticality rating: 5/10** — Confirms and formalizes existing intuitions without changing the operational picture. Valuable for theoretical completeness and manuscript writing.

## Additional Papers Examined but Not Flagged

The following papers were examined and assessed as LOW or below the flagging threshold for the core Gradience spectral analysis program. Brief notes for the record:

- **Montanari & Wang (2026), "Phase Transitions for Feature Learning in Neural Networks" ([arXiv:2602.01434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01434))** — Rigorous two-phase training dynamics (gradient-dominated → Hessian-dominated). Confirms the conceptual story behind §16a curvature telemetry but operates in a proportional-asymptotics regime (n,d → ∞) that is far from the LoRA fine-tuning setting. LOW for operational relevance; worth a footnote citation in THEORY.md §4.

- **Kalra et al. (2026), "A Scalable Measure of Loss Landscape Curvature for Analyzing the Training Dynamics of LLMs" ([arXiv:2601.16979](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16979))** — Introduces "relative critical sharpness" (λ_c), a scalable curvature proxy that captures Edge of Stability at 7B scale (OLMo-2). Methodologically adjacent to Gradience's curvature telemetry but addresses pre-training/mid-training dynamics rather than LoRA fine-tuning. LOW for integration; MEDIUM if the program ever extends curvature telemetry to larger-scale training.

- **Kang et al. (2026), "Uniform Spectral Growth and Convergence of Muon in LoRA-Style Matrix Factorization" ([arXiv:2602.06385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06385))** — Shows that the Muon optimizer produces uniform singular value growth in LoRA training, implying fundamentally different spectral profiles than AdamW-trained adapters. LOW because Muon adoption in the LoRA community is minimal; worth a note in THEORY.md §1 that spectral profiles are optimizer-dependent.

- **Kim, Kim, Ryu (2025), "LoRA Training Provably Converges to a Low-Rank Global Minimum" ([arXiv:2502.09376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09376), ICML 2025)** — Landscape analysis showing two convergence regimes (low-rank/small-magnitude vs high-rank/large-magnitude). Consistent with existing findings but no direct operational implication. LOW.

- **PARA: Post-Optimization Adaptive Rank Allocation for LoRA ([arXiv:2604.27796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27796))** — Post-hoc SVD-based rank pruning via global threshold. Validates Gradience's rank-compression premise but uses a simpler method (global threshold vs. per-layer policy). LOW for research; confirms existing findings.

- **TARA-Merging: Preference-Aligned LoRA Merging ([arXiv:2603.26299](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26299))** — Formalizes subspace coverage and directional anisotropy as merge failure modes. Conceptually aligned with Gradience's subspace overlap analysis but operates as an execution method, not triage. LOW for integration.

- **OSRM: Unraveling LoRA Interference ([arXiv:2505.22934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22934), ACL 2025)** — Constrains LoRA subspace pre-training to minimize cross-task interference. Complements Gradience's post-hoc triage from the training side. LOW — different intervention point.

- **SpectralLoRA: Is Low-Frequency Structure Sufficient? ([arXiv:2604.10649](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10649))** — DCT-domain analysis showing 33% of frequency coefficients capture 90% of energy. Operates in a different spectral domain (DCT vs SVD); confirms compressibility but via a different decomposition. LOW.

- **SpecLoRA: Weight Spectra Induced Efficient Model Adaptation ([arXiv:2505.23099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23099))** — Confirms via SVD that fine-tuning amplifies top singular values while leaving the remainder intact. Directionally consistent with N127 spectral partitioning findings but a method paper rather than an empirical characterization. LOW.

- **Xu et al. (2025), "Understanding the Learning Dynamics of LoRA: A Gradient Flow Perspective" ([arXiv:2503.06982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06982), AISTATS 2025)** — Gradient flow analysis of LoRA dynamics; convergence governed by misalignment between pre-trained and target singular spaces. Conceptually aligned but operates in the matrix-factorization-under-gradient-flow regime rather than the post-hoc audit regime. LOW.
