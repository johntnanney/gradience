### Brittlebench: Quantifying LLM Robustness via Prompt Sensitivity (Romanou, Ibrahim, Ross, Shaib, Oktar, Bell, Ovalle, Dodge, Bosselut, Sinha, Williams) — HIGH

**Authors / venue / date:** Angelika Romanou (EPFL), Mark Ibrahim, Candace Ross, Chantal Shaib, Kerem Oktar, Samuel J. Bell, Anaelia Ovalle, Jesse Dodge, Antoine Bosselut, Koustuv Sinha, Adina Williams (Meta FAIR + EPFL + Allen AI). arXiv preprint, **v1 submitted 2026-02-27, last revised 2026-04-06 (v2)** — three weeks before today's review. CC-BY-SA 4.0 licensed. No journal venue stated yet.

**arXiv / DOI:** [arXiv:2603.13285](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13285) · [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2603.13285](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2603.13285) · [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.13285v2)

**One-sentence summary:** Introduces a theoretical framework for quantifying LLM "brittleness" — model sensitivity to semantics-preserving prompt perturbations — applies it to popular benchmarks across frontier open-weight and commercial models, and decomposes total performance variance, finding that perturbations account for up to half of a model's variance and that even a single perturbation alters the relative ranking between two models in 63% of cases.

**Connection to program:** Direct overlap with the benchmark reliability study's three primary hypotheses. (i) **H1 (tolerance schedule):** the paper's "up to 12% performance degradation" and "perturbations account for up to half of total variance" are within the same epistemic register as the benchmark study's tolerance-schedule contribution; the paper estimates a similar quantity (variance attributable to admissible-input variation) on a different but adjacent measurement universe (semantics-preserving perturbations vs. community-sourced prompts × seeds × scoring rules). (ii) **H3 (ranking stability):** the paper's headline finding that "even a single perturbation alters the relative ranking of models in 63% of cases" is directly comparable to H3, which sets a 20% threshold for ranking-reversal fraction. The 63% figure on frontier-models-and-popular-benchmarks is a rough upper-envelope estimate of what the benchmark study expects to find on small-to-mid-open-models with admissible-prompt variance — the orders of magnitude are consistent with the study's hypothesis that single-occasion ranking claims are unstable. (iii) **Variance-decomposition vocabulary:** the paper "decomposes total variance" of model performance, paralleling Analysis 1's variance-components decomposition. The author list includes well-established eval-methodology names (Jesse Dodge, Adina Williams, Antoine Bosselut), which is meaningful pedigree for the benchmark study's positioning.

**Why this criticality tier:** HIGH, not CRITICAL. The benchmark reliability study's distinctive prescriptive contribution — the **decimal-place precision tolerance schedule** under §3.5 — is not formulated in Brittlebench, which targets *brittleness as a model property* via theoretical decomposition rather than *measurement-condition tolerance as a benchmark-cell property* via G-theory variance components. The two are complementary: Brittlebench frames prompt sensitivity as a model-level brittleness diagnostic; the benchmark study frames the same phenomenon as a measurement-tolerance issue requiring decimal-place reporting discipline. Empirical-scope non-overlap is also clean: Brittlebench evaluates frontier open-weight + commercial models and uses adversarial-flavored semantics-preserving perturbations (typos, paraphrase); the benchmark study evaluates small-to-mid open models and uses *community-provenance* admissible prompts (benchmark authors, lm-eval-harness, HELM, minimal-direct) without adversarial perturbations. Brittlebench therefore strengthens rather than subsumes the benchmark study's framing, but a reviewer of the benchmark study who is familiar with this paper will expect to see it cited and distinguished. The paper is sufficiently recent (v2 2026-04-06) and methodologically close that **the manuscript's related-work and Analysis 4 (ranking stability) discussion will need to engage it explicitly**.

**Recommended action:** Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` Section 4 as **HIGH** (parallel-development, must-cite for benchmark reliability study; nice-to-have for N134 if revision space permits — Brittlebench is methodologically adjacent to the construct-validity register N134 invokes, but a single mention in N134's prompt-sensitivity-literature paragraph is sufficient). Add to manuscript citation list for the benchmark reliability study (introduction §2.1, related work, §7.4 ranking-stability discussion). The benchmark study's H3 discussion gains a useful comparison anchor: Brittlebench reports 63% pairwise reversal under any-single-perturbation; the benchmark study's H3 threshold is 20% under condition resampling. The two figures are not directly comparable (different perturbation universes, different model regimes), but the order-of-magnitude framing helps. **Do not modify** the v1.1.2 pre-registration.

**Framework-applied note:** Brittlebench reports point estimates (12% degradation, 63% ranking reversal, "up to half" of variance) without explicit reliability-across-seed evidence in the abstract, and the variance-decomposition methodology is not specified at the abstract level. A measurement-discipline-applied scrutiny would ask: are the 12% / 63% / 50% figures themselves reported with cross-seed reliability or bootstrap CIs? Does the variance decomposition use a binomial-family GLMM (per NIST AI 800-3's endorsement) or a linear approximation? Full-text review would be required to answer; the abstract's framing is consistent with a paper that *could* meet measurement-discipline standards but does not foreground the meta-measurement layer the framework would expect. This is not a flaw — Brittlebench is appropriately scoped — but it suggests that a recursive framework-applied note in the benchmark study's discussion of Brittlebench is methodologically generative rather than merely citational.

### Sui et al. (2025), *Evaluating large language models as raters in large-scale writing assessments: A psychometric framework for reliability and validity* — MEDIUM (promotion from OUTSTANDING)

**Authors / venue / date:** Sui et al. **Computers and Education: Artificial Intelligence** (Elsevier), publication date 2025-09-25. Article identifier `S2666920X25001213`. Original paper remains paywalled; a companion **public GitHub repository** ([John-Wang-0809/LLM-Writing-Assessment-Psychometric-Framework](https://github.com/John-Wang-0809/LLM-Writing-Assessment-Psychometric-Framework)) confirms the methodology and substrate.

**arXiv / DOI:** [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666920X25001213) (paywalled; abstract publicly visible). Companion code: [GitHub](https://github.com/John-Wang-0809/LLM-Writing-Assessment-Psychometric-Framework).

**One-sentence summary:** Applies generalizability theory and many-facet Rasch modeling to compare human and LLM raters across three essay genres on 4,315 samples, finding that human-LLM discrepancies stem from fundamental evaluation differences (humans use broad-spectrum holistic judgment, LLMs excel at fine-grained narrow-interval evaluation), with Claude models showing superior scoring stability over GPT.

**Connection to program:** This paper was flagged 2026-04-25 inaugural pass as **OUTSTANDING** because the abstract was retrievable but the body was paywall-blocked, and was identified as "potentially CRITICAL" given the explicit G-theory + MFRM methodology. Two follow-up retrieval attempts (yesterday inaugural pass; yesterday second pass) failed; today's pass located a companion GitHub repository whose README confirms the methodological details — generalizability theory, many-facet Rasch modeling, three essay genres, 4,315 samples, comparing human vs. LLM raters. With the methodology now confirmable from the GitHub repo, the paper's positioning is clearer: **the inferential target is LLM-as-rater of human writing** (essay scoring), not LLM-as-subject answering benchmarks. The methodological vocabulary is identical to the benchmark study's (G-theory, MFRM is a stronger psychometric tool than the benchmark study's CTT-flavored G-theory variance components), but the substrate differs in a way that matters: Sui et al. study LLMs as evaluation instruments for human-produced text; the benchmark study studies benchmark items as evaluation instruments for LLMs. Both are measurement-discipline applications; the inferential pipelines do not overlap directly.

**Why this criticality tier:** MEDIUM (promotion from OUTSTANDING). The criticality is downgraded from "potentially CRITICAL" to MEDIUM after substrate clarification: Sui et al. is a methodological cousin on a different inferential target, not a competing or superseding paper on the benchmark study's substrate. The methodological convergence (G-theory, MFRM applied to LLMs) is striking and worth citing — it strengthens the parallel-development register the inventory has been tracking — but it does not affect the benchmark study's contribution claim or protocol. The N134 paper's internal_summary names IRT-style decomposition explicitly as future-work; MFRM is one such tool, and Sui et al. is now the canonical recent reference for MFRM-applied-to-LLMs (on the rater substrate). For the benchmark study's manuscript, Sui et al. is a one-paragraph or one-sentence engagement: "Sui et al. (2025) apply G-theory and many-facet Rasch modeling to LLM-as-rater scoring of human writing; the substrate differs from benchmark accuracy as measurement, but the methodological convergence demonstrates the portability of the G-theory register across LLM-evaluation contexts." Not action-forcing for protocol or contribution.

**Recommended action:** **Promote** from `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` Section 6 (OUTSTANDING) to Section 4 — Tracked parallel work — at MEDIUM tier. Cite-and-distinguish in the benchmark reliability study's §2 or §13 (Relationship-to-N134 / Future Work). Cite in N134's IRT-extension future-work paragraph (§strategic in internal_summary, when promoted to manuscript). The companion GitHub repo is a reasonable secondary source if the paper itself remains paywalled at submission time; institutional access via .edu library proxy remains the cleanest path. **No protocol implications.**

**Framework-applied note:** Sui et al. uses MFRM, which is methodologically richer than the benchmark study's CTT-flavored G-theory (Brennan 2001 register). MFRM separates rater severity, item difficulty, and ratee ability simultaneously — a capability the benchmark study's variance-components decomposition does not have because it operates on a different unit (benchmark item × measurement condition, not rater × ratee × occasion). The recursive framework check would not flag Sui et al. — the paper is methodologically careful and uses well-established psychometric tools. The framework-applied opportunity is in the *other* direction: MFRM is a strictly stronger tool than CTT for many evaluation problems, and a future iteration of the benchmark study could adopt MFRM if the protocol is ever revisited beyond v1.1.2-LOCKED. Recording this for the IRT-extension future-work register.
