### 1. Paul (2026), *Spectral Geometry of LoRA Adapters Encodes Training Objective and Predicts Harmful Compliance* — HIGH (approaching MUST-CITE)

**Authors / venue / date:** Roi Paul. arXiv preprint, submitted **2026-04-12** (arXiv:2604.08844). No venue stated.

**arXiv:** [arXiv:2604.08844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08844) · [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08844) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08844)

**One-sentence summary:** In a pre-registered experiment on Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct with 38 manufactured LoRA adapters across four categories (healthy SFT, DPO on inverted harmlessness preferences, DPO on inverted helpfulness preferences, activation-steering-derived), the paper shows that low-rank spectral summaries of LoRA weight deltas — using norms, stable rank, singular-value entropy, effective rank, and singular-vector cosine alignment — can perfectly classify training objectives within a single method (AUC 1.00) and predict downstream behavioral harm with ρ = 0.72.

**Connection to program:**

This is the single most program-relevant paper that has appeared during the review gap. It validates three core premises of the Gradience framework simultaneously:

1. **Same spectral features.** The paper uses exactly the metrics Gradience computes in `vnext/audit/lora_audit.py`: stable rank, effective rank, singular-value entropy, spectral norms. This is independent convergent validation that these features encode meaningful information about adapter behavior — the paper's result that PC1 in flattened weight-delta space perfectly separates training objectives (AUC 1.00) is a stronger version of the claim underlying Gradience's spectral triage system.

2. **Spectral-to-behavioral bridge.** The geometry-to-behavior rank correlation (ρ = 0.72) across 24 non-steered adapters directly addresses the "behavioral bridge" problem that the Gradience program has been tracking (see `docs/research/behavioral_bridges.md`). The QA artifact system's `behavioral_summary` field documents evidence and `eligibility` encodes policy judgment — Paul's result provides empirical evidence that spectral geometry carries behavioral signal, which is the inferential leap Gradience's adapter QA artifact relies on.

3. **Critical negative result.** Cross-method generalization failed completely (DPO-trained classifier assigned every steering adapter a lower drift score than every DPO adapter, AUC 0.00). This is load-bearing for Gradience's own negative finding at N134 decoder scale (spectral triage for per-pair merge prediction does not survive confound control). The within-method/cross-method boundary aligns with Gradience's finding that spectral profiles encode structural properties but do not generalize across training paradigms without method-specific calibration.

**Why this criticality tier:** HIGH approaching MUST-CITE. For N134: the geometry-to-behavior correlation (ρ = 0.72) and the cross-method generalization failure (AUC 0.00) are both independently relevant to N134's framing. The cross-method failure is essentially the same structural finding as N134's negative result — spectral features are locally informative but do not survive substrate shifts — and citing Paul's convergent negative result strengthens N134's claim that this limitation is fundamental, not artifactual. For the spectral-audit module: the feature set convergence (same metrics, independent researcher) is external validation of the measurement instruments Gradience implements. **Approaching MUST-CITE because**: (a) independent pre-registered experiment using the same spectral feature vocabulary; (b) strongest published evidence for the spectral-behavioral bridge that Gradience's QA artifact relies on; (c) convergent negative result that supports N134's thesis about measurement-discipline limitations. **Not yet MUST-CITE because**: (a) single-author preprint without peer review; (b) single model (Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct) with manufactured adapters rather than wild-sourced; (c) the 38-adapter scope is small. If this appears at a venue (workshop or above), promote to MUST-CITE.

**Relevance to Gradience technical framework: CRITICAL.** The paper validates the spectral feature vocabulary Gradience has built its tooling around. The cross-method failure result may warrant a note in `docs/THEORY.md` acknowledging that spectral geometry is method-conditioned — a theoretical constraint the framework should make explicit.

**Recommended action:** Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` Section 4 at HIGH. Flag for N134 first-revision bibliography pass (cite as convergent validation of spectral-geometry informativeness + convergent negative on cross-method generalization). Flag for Gradience library documentation as external validation of the spectral audit feature set.

### 2. Wei et al. (2026), *SSR-Merge: Subspace Signal Routing for Training-Free LoRA Merging in Diffusion Models* — HIGH

**Authors / venue / date:** Zhengxuan Wei, Yi Dong, Zonghui Li, Xianhui Lin, Xing Liu, Hong Gu, Shaofeng Zhang, Wenbin Li, Qi Fan. **ICML 2026** (accepted). arXiv preprint submitted **2026-06-16** (arXiv:2606.10617).

**arXiv:** [arXiv:2606.10617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10617) · [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.10617) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.10617) · [GitHub](https://github.com/nagara214/SSR-Merge)

**One-sentence summary:** Recasts LoRA merging as signal routing in a unified low-rank subspace — concatenating candidate LoRAs along the rank dimension, decorrelating mixed signals via an inverse correlation matrix, and steering purified signals into task-specific subspaces — with a theoretical proof that the routing aligns with the OLS solution, achieving mathematical optimality.

**Connection to program:**

This paper is directly relevant to Gradience's merge pipeline (`vnext/merge/`) and merge compatibility analysis:

1. **Subspace-routing vs parameter-arithmetic framing.** Gradience's merge pipeline diagnoses pairs as SAFE/REDUNDANT/CONFLICTING/IMBALANCED based on spectral compatibility metrics (subspace overlap, norm ratio, spectral gap). SSR-Merge's theoretical contribution — that merge interference is better understood as signal-routing in a unified subspace rather than parameter-space arithmetic — provides a formal mathematical framework that complements Gradience's empirical diagnostic vocabulary. The "unified subspace by concatenating along the rank dimension" construction is algebraically related to Gradience's subspace overlap computation but arrives at an optimal routing rather than a diagnostic label.

2. **Theoretical optimality guarantees.** The OLS-optimality proof establishes that SSR's routing minimizes reconstruction error in the least-squares sense. This is a stronger theoretical result than Gradience's heuristic strategy recommendations (linear, ties, dare_ties, dare_linear, norm_equalized, audit_aware), and may inform whether Gradience's strategy-selection logic could be improved by incorporating OLS-optimal routing as a reference strategy.

3. **Diffusion-model substrate limitation.** The empirical validation is on diffusion models (Stable Diffusion, FLUX), not LLMs. This is a substrate mismatch with Gradience's primary focus (LLM adapters). The theoretical result (OLS optimality) is substrate-agnostic, but the empirical validation does not directly confirm LLM applicability. The program should track whether LLM follow-up experiments appear.

**Why this criticality tier:** HIGH. ICML 2026 acceptance gives this paper high epistemic weight. The theoretical framework (subspace signal routing with OLS-optimal guarantees) is a genuine advance over the parameter-arithmetic framing that most merge methods (Task Arithmetic, TIES, DARE) use, and that Gradience's strategy recommendations build on. However, the diffusion-model substrate means the empirical results don't directly validate LLM merge scenarios. **Not MUST-CITE because**: (a) substrate mismatch (diffusion models vs. LLMs); (b) the theoretical contribution is complementary to, not supplanting, Gradience's diagnostic approach (diagnosis vs. prescription); (c) N134's merge-substrate is deemphasized. **HIGH because**: (a) ICML 2026 venue; (b) the subspace-routing framing is the most theoretically principled merge formulation published to date; (c) the unified-subspace construction is algebraically related to Gradience's spectral analysis.

**Relevance to Gradience technical framework: HIGH.** The OLS-optimal routing could inform future strategy-selection improvements in `vnext/merge/`. The subspace construction (concatenation along rank dimension + decorrelation) is a natural extension of Gradience's subspace overlap computation. If the program ever revisits the merge substrate, SSR is the reference method to benchmark against.

**Recommended action:** Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` Section 4 at HIGH (merge-substrate, but theoretically principled). Monitor for LLM follow-up experiments.

### 3. (SpectralLoRA) *Is Low-Frequency Structure Sufficient for LoRA Adaptation? A Spectral Analysis of Weight Updates* — MEDIUM

**Authors / venue / date:** Authors not retrieved in search results. arXiv preprint submitted **2026-04-24** (arXiv:2604.10649). No venue stated.

**arXiv:** [arXiv:2604.10649](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10649) · [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10649) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.10649)

**One-sentence summary:** A systematic empirical study establishing that LoRA weight updates are universally low-frequency in the 2D DCT (Discrete Cosine Transform) domain — on average, 33% of DCT coefficients capture 90% of spectral energy across BERT-base and RoBERTa-base on four GLUE benchmarks — with an SVD-DCT correlation of Pearson r = 0.906 (p < 10^-9) connecting the empirical constant to SGD spectral dynamics.

**Connection to program:**

1. **Energy concentration mirrors Gradience's findings.** The 90% energy threshold in SpectralLoRA directly mirrors Gradience's `energy_rank_90` metric (the minimal k such that top-k singular values carry >= 90% Frobenius energy). While SpectralLoRA uses DCT coefficients and Gradience uses singular values, both arrive at the same structural observation: LoRA updates concentrate their informative content in a small fraction of their allocated capacity. The SVD-DCT correlation (r = 0.906) bridges these two views, providing theoretical justification for why Gradience's SVD-based approach captures the same structure that a frequency-domain analysis reveals.

2. **Rank utilization convergence.** SpectralLoRA's finding that "just 33% of DCT coefficients capture 90% of total spectral energy" is the frequency-domain analog of Gradience's finding that mean rank utilization is 0.172 (approximately 1/6 of allocated rank used). The two measures are computed on different mathematical objects (DCT spectrum vs. singular value spectrum) but converge on the same structural claim: LoRA adapters are grossly over-parameterized relative to their effective information content.

3. **Model-dependent compressibility.** SpectralLoRA finds "RoBERTa-base is systematically more spectrally compressible than BERT-base across all tasks." This provides independent evidence for Gradience's observation that spectral profiles are model-dependent (though Gradience's finding was that profiles are "architecture-agnostic in practice" for audit purposes — there may be a useful distinction between "architecture-agnostic for diagnostic thresholds" vs. "architecture-dependent for compression ratios").

4. **Encoder-only substrate limitation.** The empirical scope is BERT-base and RoBERTa-base on GLUE tasks — encoder-only models on classification benchmarks. Gradience's primary substrate has shifted to decoder-scale models (Mistral-7B, Llama). The findings may not transfer directly to decoder architectures, though the SVD-DCT correlation as a theoretical relationship should be architecture-agnostic.

**Why this criticality tier:** MEDIUM. The SVD-DCT correlation is a genuine theoretical contribution that bridges Gradience's SVD analysis to a frequency-domain understanding, potentially providing a complementary theoretical grounding for the energy-concentration phenomena Gradience measures. However: (a) the encoder-only substrate is not the program's current focus; (b) the core observation (LoRA updates are over-parameterized) is already well-established in Gradience's findings; (c) the DCT-domain analysis adds a complementary view but does not change diagnostic methodology. **Not HIGH because** the contribution is confirmatory rather than novel from the Gradience program's perspective — it validates what Gradience already measures, rather than revealing something new. **MEDIUM because** the SVD-DCT bridge (r = 0.906) is a useful theoretical connection that could strengthen the theoretical grounding in `docs/THEORY.md`, particularly the section on spectral structure foundations.

**Relevance to Gradience technical framework: MODERATE.** The SVD-DCT correlation could be cited as theoretical support for why SVD-based energy concentration metrics work. No code changes are implied.

**Recommended action:** Add to `RESEARCH_INVENTORY.md` Section 4 at MEDIUM. Cite-if-natural in `docs/THEORY.md` spectral structure section as independent theoretical bridge between SVD and frequency-domain characterizations.
