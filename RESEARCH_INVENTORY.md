# Research Inventory

**Status:** living document seeded 2026-04-25. Tracks external work the program has either cited, plans to cite, or is watching for relevance to the current research lines.

**How this is maintained:** the daily research review (`research_review/daily_review_prompt.md`) flags new candidates in dated reports under `research_review/`. Promotion of a candidate from a daily report into this inventory is a human decision — the daily reviewer recommends; this file records what the program has accepted as worth tracking.

**How to read criticality tiers:**

- **MUST-CITE** — load-bearing for at least one current or in-prep manuscript. Cannot be omitted from related work without an explicit reason.
- **HIGH** — parallel-development work or methodologically adjacent. Must be engaged in related-work / discussion sections of in-prep manuscripts.
- **MEDIUM** — adjacent literature consolidating in a register the program engages with. Track-and-cite-if-natural; not action-forcing.
- **LOW** — methodologically related but on a substrate the program has deemphasized, or confirmatory rather than novel. Watch-only.
- **OUTSTANDING** — flagged but not yet retrievable or not yet adequately assessed. Re-evaluate on a future daily review pass.

## Section 8 — Notes for manuscript writing

These observations have accumulated across daily reviews and are worth surfacing when manuscript-revision time arrives. Treat as candidate-content for the §2 or §3.1 framing of either paper.

1. **Parallel-development register.** The program is no longer the only one applying measurement-decomposition to LLM evaluation. Across roughly twelve months, the field has produced **nine convergent voices**: Messing (2026) TEE framework for LLM evaluation pipelines; NIST AI 800-2/800-3 voluntary practices and GLMM endorsement; Bean et al. NeurIPS 2025 construct-validity review with 445-paper systematic taxonomy; Camuffo et al. (2026) variance-aware LLM annotation in strategy research; Romanou et al. (2026) Brittlebench prompt-brittleness decomposition; Heineman et al. (2025) Signal and Noise framework on 30 benchmarks × 375 models; McGregor et al. (NeurIPS 2025) BenchRisk risk-management framework on 26 benchmarks × 57 failure modes × 196 mitigations; Lior et al. (Findings of EMNLP 2025) ReliableEval method-of-moments resampling recipe for stochastic LLM evaluation on five frontier LLMs; Salaudeen et al. (2025) validity-centered framework adapting psychometrics' breakdown of validity to AI evaluation. The N134 manuscript's framing as "first to propose" must be retired entirely (not merely softened) in favor of "co-developed register, distinctive prescriptive contribution." The load-bearing differentiator is the **decimal-place precision tolerance schedule** as the prescriptive output — Messing's TEE optimizes budget allocation, NIST 800-3 endorses GLMM methodology, the construct-validity review provides a taxonomy, Camuffo et al. develop variance-aware annotation protocols, Brittlebench frames model-level brittleness, Signal and Noise produces benchmark-design intervention recommendations, BenchRisk produces a scored metaevaluation benchmark, ReliableEval produces a sample-size determination rule, Salaudeen articulates a validity-centered framework — but none formulates a tolerance schedule that licenses decimal-place precision in reported scores. That is the contribution the program still owns. **Vocabulary note:** "signal" (in the Heineman et al. register) maps onto "generalizability coefficient" (in the G-theory register the program uses) — same quantity, different methodological tradition; ReliableEval's method-of-moments resampling produces, in the limit, a frequentist standard error on the same population mean that G-theory's universe-score variance estimates — a third estimation tradition converging on the same target. The benchmark-reliability §3.5 paragraph should acknowledge this convergence rather than reinventing the vocabulary. **NIST-alignment note:** BenchRisk applies the NIST risk management process; the benchmark study's regime-split (D-09 → v1.1.2) responds to NIST AI 800-3's GLMM endorsement; the program's NIST-alignment is increasingly load-bearing as the field's voluntary-standards register consolidates. **Reuel-author-thread note:** Anka Reuel co-authors three of the nine voices (Bean, McGregor, Salaudeen) at three distinct conceptual levels (failure-mode taxonomy, risk-management metaevaluation, validity-framework articulation); the clustering is itself a register signal worth tracking. **N134 vs. benchmark-reliability submission state:** the N134 v3 tarball ships the 8-voice claim as accurate-at-time-of-submission (Salaudeen surfaced post-tarball-build); the benchmark-reliability paper engages all nine voices because Salaudeen surfaced before its tarball assembly. **Dissenting-voice update (2026-05-03 second pass):** Hua, Tang, Gu, Gu, Wong, Qin (EMNLP 2025) *Flaw or Artifact? Rethinking Prompt Sensitivity in Evaluating LLMs* (arXiv:2509.01790) is the corpus's first explicitly contrarian voice — argues that much prompt sensitivity is artifact-of-evaluation (LL scoring + rigid answer matching) rather than real model property, and that LLM-as-Judge substantially reduces measured variance and stabilizes rankings. The corpus's epistemic state thus becomes **nine convergent + one dissenting**, which is a stronger position than nine-voice silent convergence: *engaged-and-survived dissent makes the convergence-claim falsifiable in a way pure agreement does not*. Engagement is deferred to the benchmark-reliability paper's first-revision pass (paper-as-submitted ships the 9-voice convergence claim accurate-at-time-of-submission); the first-revision §2.1 / §7.4 engagement should distinguish three structural grounds the paper's claim survives Hua et al. on (sequencing-not-conflict, regime-split-already-handles-artifact, construct-articulation-not-capability-readout) rather than litigate the artifact-vs-property question on Hua et al.'s home turf. The N134 paper does not need to engage Hua et al. unless it goes through first-revision; it does not make claims at the prompt-sensitivity level Hua et al. challenge.

2. **Recursive framework application.** The program's framework can be applied as a critical instrument to the papers in this inventory. Specifically: Messing's "halves estimation error" claim is reported as a percentage gain, not a bounded interval — a framework-applied scrutiny would request reliability evidence on the small-sample pilot itself. This is the "framework-as-meta-tool" angle a future paper could exploit (analyzing recent ML-eval methods under measurement-discipline standards).

3. **Substrate-deemphasis defensibility.** The program's decision to deemphasize the merge substrate is increasingly visible in the inventory: most LoRA-merge work (Pico, LoRI, DO-Merging, etc.) sits in Section 4-5 territory rather than Section 2's must-cite. The N134 paper's discussion should name this shift explicitly — the merge-line is not "abandoned"; it's "negatively settled at decoder scale, with the framework now applied to a different substrate."

4. **Philosophical-engagement target.** Freiesleben (2026) is the cleanest external philosophical engagement with the measurement-discipline framework that has surfaced to date — argues for Cronbach-Meehl nomological-network construct validity over Messick's inferential account specifically for LLM construct-validity work. N134 §2 invokes Messick implicitly; the framework's choice between accounts is a live debate Freiesleben represents one position in. This is now the named interlocutor for the philosophical-companion-paper thread (per N134 `internal_summary.md` "Philosophical" subsection of "Next implications"). The transcendental-argument and two-kinds-of-findings threads both engage construct-validity philosophy directly; Freiesleben's framing makes the companion paper materially more concrete than it was without an external position to engage.
